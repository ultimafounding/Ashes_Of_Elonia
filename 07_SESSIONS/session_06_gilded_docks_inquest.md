## 2. Recap and the Cliff (15 min)

Open on the cliff top, wet, exhausted, with the Codex. The signal flare is still in the sky.

> *The flare hangs over the bay like a pin stuck in the dark. Below, the sea is eating the stair. Behind, the cave is full of noise — stone, water, and someone shouting your names.*

**Ask the table:**

- *"Who is carrying the Codex?"*
- *"Who is hurt?"*
- *"Where is Tamsin?"*
- *"What did you leave in the chamber?"*

**If Kael survived and is free:** his voice carries up the stair: *"You forgot the strongbox!"* He is not chasing yet. He is salvaging.
**If Kael was taken prisoner:** he is with the party, bound and furious, and the Cartels below are leaderless. Decide whether he is a witness or a liability.
**If Kael died:** the Cartels are leaderless for now. Rorik will hear.
**If the Cartels are at max interference:** a second lantern appears on the road below. They were waiting.

## 3. The Escape (35 min)

### 3.1 The chase

The party must get off the cliff before the tide or the Cartels catch them. Use a **chase** (three rounds) or a **skill challenge** (3 successes before 2 failures).

**Chase participants:**

- **The party**, carrying the Codex (25 lb; disadvantage on Acrobatics if carried in hand).
- **A Cartel pursuit** — 2 Thugs and a Scout, or Kael if he survived. They are not trying to kill the party; they are trying to take the Codex.
- **The tide**, rising behind them.

**Chase rules (simplified):**

- Each round, everyone makes a **DC 12 Dexterity (Acrobatics) or Strength (Athletics)** check. Success: move one zone. Failure: lose ground; the pursuers close.
- **Zones:** the sea cave → the cliff stair → the cliff road → the mule/pony → the South Gate.
- **Complications:** a rope bridge, a collapsed stair, a Cartel ambush, a tide surge.

**If the pursuit catches them:** they can fight, drop the Codex, or try a **DC 14 Deception** to make the Cartels think the Codex fell into the sea.

### 3.2 The cliff stair

The stair is slick and half-flooded. **DC 13 Athletics** to climb without slowing. A PC carrying the Codex has **disadvantage** unless someone else takes it or they use a rope.

**If Tamsin is with them:** he is slow. A PC can help him (**DC 12 Athletics**), or leave him to follow. If they leave him, he arrives later, hurt and quiet.

**If Toby is with them:** he knows a **fisherman's path** (DC 12 Survival) that bypasses the worst of the stair. He offers it if they were kind to him.

### 3.3 The Cartel pursuit

The Cartels want the Codex, not a body count. They will:

- **Demand the Codex** (DC 13 Intimidation/Persuasion to stall).
- **Try to cut the rope** or block the path.
- **Offer a deal:** *"Give us the book. Rorik will pay you instead of hunting you."*

**If the party drops the Codex:** the Cartels stop chasing. The party escapes, but loses the chapter's prize. Seraphel still intervenes, but the fellowship is conditional.

**If the party fights:** use the Thug/Scout blocks. Kael, if present, fights defensively and withdraws at half HP.

### 3.4 The mule / pony

If they kept **Bramble** the mule, it is on the cliff top, panicking. **DC 12 Animal Handling** to calm it. It can carry the Codex and a wounded ally, but it slows them on the stair.

If they lost the mule, they walk. The road back is **four hours**.

### 3.5 The signal flare

The flare was **violet**, not Admiralty white. **DC 13 Arcana/Investigation:** it is an alchemical signal, expensive, and not a Cartel colour. `[DM-ONLY: Seraphel's people, watching. Do not confirm.]`

If the party investigates, they find a **brass casing** on the cliff with a **falcon and calipers** stamp. That is the clearest physical trace of Seraphel in the campaign so far — the road coin, if they found it, was easier to dismiss.

## 4. The Road Back (25 min)

### 4.1 Travel

The road back is the same six miles, but the weather has turned. Roll or choose:

| d6 | Weather | Effect |
|----|---------|--------|
| 1–2 | **Rain** | Tracks wash away; the road is slick; camp is miserable |
| 3–4 | **Gusting** | Disadvantage on ranged attacks; loose gear must be secured |
| 5–6 | **Clear, cold** | Good visibility; the stars are very bright |

**Travel encounter (choose one):**

- **An Admiralty patrol** — they have orders to watch the road. **DC 13 Deception/Persuasion** to pass, or a bribe (20 gp), or a chase.
- **A Cartel straggler** — a wounded Thug from the Star Chamber. He can be questioned, healed, or left.
- **A fisher family** — Toby's family, if he was rescued. They offer food, dry clothes, and a place to rest.
- **A shrine to Myrraen** — a quiet scene; a PC can pray, leave an offering, or repair the niche.

### 4.2 Wounded and tired

If the party is low on HP or resources, let them take a **short rest** on the road. It costs time, not safety. If they push on without resting, they arrive at the Inquest with **one level of exhaustion** (disadvantage on ability checks), which makes the trial harder.

**Do not** punish them for resting. The Inquest is the challenge, not the road.

### 4.3 Tamsin's conversation

If Tamsin is with them, he asks the question he has been avoiding:

> *"Was it worth it? Wren died for a room full of paper. Tell me it was worth it."*

**How the party answers matters.** There is no check. Listen. If they honour Wren, Tamsin steadies. If they dismiss him, he withdraws.

**What Tamsin wants:**

- To be useful at the Inquest (he can testify about the Cartels).
- To see Rorik pay.
- To know that Wren's death meant something.

**If they promise him a place in the story:** he tells them everything he knows about Rorik's operation, including the **name of the Cartel's contact in the Admiralty** (a clerk named **Osk**). That is a real edge in the trial.

### 4.4 The Codex on the road

The Codex is vulnerable. Rain, sea spray, and a fall can damage it.

- **Waterproof map case** (bought in Session 4): no risk.
- **No case:** **DC 12 Survival** to keep it dry; failure damages one excerpt (choose one).
- **Dropped in the chase:** the party must decide whether to go back. If they do, they lose an hour and face a Cartel patrol.

## 5. Port Meridian and the Inquest (90 min)

### 5.1 The return

Port Meridian is grey and busy. The news has beaten them: a **salvage dispute** at Warehouse Nine, a **Breaker in the cells**, and a **black stone** that the Admiralty wants to see.

**At the gate:**

- **Admiralty guards** are checking salvage. **DC 12 Deception** to hide the Codex, or **DC 13 Persuasion** to declare it and ask for a receipt.
- **A runner** from Helena finds them: *"She says to come to the warehouse. Now. And bring the book."*
- **A poster** on the gatehouse wall: *"UNLICENSED ANTIQUARIANISM — NOTICE OF INQUEST."* The party's names are not on it yet. They will be by noon.

### 5.2 The charges

At the Gilded Docks court, **Magistrate Ilya Renn** reads the charges:

1. **Unlicensed antiquarianism** — recovery of a protected antiquity without a scholarly writ.
2. **Failure to declare** — the Codex was not logged with the Admiralty within one day.
3. **Obstruction** — the party fled the site and resisted Cartel recovery (the Cartels have friends in the court).
4. **Salvage fraud** — using a salvage charter to cover an antiquity.

**Penalty:** a fine of **500 gp** or **impressment** into the naval salvage corps for one year. The Codex is impounded pending a ruling.

**DM note:** the charges are real. The party did break the law. The question is whether the law is just, and who can afford to argue.

### 5.3 Evidence

The party can gather evidence before the hearing. Each piece is a short scene and gives **advantage or a bonus** in the trial.

| Evidence | Where | Check / scene | Benefit |
|----------|-------|---------------|---------|
| **Helena's charter log** | Warehouse Nine | Helena gives it if they bring the Codex | Advantage on the legal argument |
| **The Cartel ledger** | Session 5 | Investigation DC 12 to organise it | Proves a criminal rival operation; +1 success |
| **Kael's testimony** | If alive | Persuasion DC 14, or a deal | Proves the Cartels were looting, not salvaging |
| **The captured Breaker** | Citadel lockup | Persuasion DC 12 + a promise | Proves the Cartel route and Rorik's orders |
| **Arlen's testimony** | Brass Astrolabe | Persuasion DC 13, or an apology | Proves the Exchange suppresses evidence |
| **Sella's records** | Brass Astrolabe | Persuasion DC 14 or 50 gp | Proves the Codex's provenance |
| **Toby's testimony** | Fisher village | No check, if rescued | Humanises the party; +1 success |
| **Tamsin's testimony** | Anywhere | No check, if trusted | Proves Wren was left to die; emotional weight |
| **The star-chart cylinder** | Session 5 | Arcana DC 14 | Suggests the Codex is part of a larger system; +1 success if presented well |
| **The violet flare casing** | Cliff | Investigation DC 13 | Links the Cartels to a wealthy patron; risky, not conclusive |

**Rule:** the party does not need every piece. Three good pieces and a decent argument can win.

### 5.3b Evidence-gathering scenes

Do not just hand out the evidence. Make the party **go get it**. Each scene is 5–10 minutes and gives them something to do before the trial.

#### A. Helena at the warehouse
Helena is packing the copper into crates, her ledger open, a bottle of bad wine at her elbow. She is angry, not at the party, but at the situation.

**What she gives:** her charter log, if they bring the Codex and tell her the truth. **No check** if they have been honest; **DC 13 Persuasion** if they hid it.

**What she wants:** a promise that they will not make her the villain of the story. *"I gave you a charter, not a licence to rob a library. But I'm not letting Rorik sell the city's memory by the pound."*

**Bonus:** if they apologise for the lie, she offers to testify in person. That is **advantage on Phase 1**.

#### B. Arlen at the Astrolabe
Arlen is in the back room, packing folios into a crate. He has not slept. He looks at them like they are a headache he cannot put down.

**What he gives:** testimony about the Exchange's suppression order — if they ask without mocking him. **DC 13 Persuasion**, or **automatic** if they apologise for how they treated him.

**What he wants:** to be believed, and to be left alone. *"I am not your friend. I am a witness. There is a difference."*

**Bonus:** if they offer to protect him from the Exchange, he gives them the **back-room folio's location** — a Chapter 3 hook.

**If they mocked him in Session 3:** he refuses. They must find another witness or proceed without him.

#### C. Sella at the counter
Sella is appraising a crate of pre-Silence glass. She does not look up when they enter.

**What she gives:** provenance for the Codex — **DC 14 Persuasion** or **50 gp**. She will testify that it is genuine and that the Cartels have been buying star-glass for months.

**What she wants:** anonymity. *"I will say what I saw. I will not say who I am afraid of."*

**Bonus:** if they pay her fairly, she sends a warning before Act II: *"The gold man knows your names."*

#### D. The captured Breaker in the citadel
The cell is cold. He is wrapped in a blanket they gave him, if they gave him one. He is waiting for a sentence.

**What he gives:** testimony about Rorik's orders and the "gold man" — if they kept their promise. **No check** if they brought him a healer or a blanket; **DC 12 Persuasion** otherwise.

**What he wants:** a reduced sentence, or at least a message sent to his sister. *"I told you what I know. Now tell them I helped."*

**Bonus:** he names **Osk**, the Cartel contact in the Admiralty. That is **+1 automatic success** in Phase 4 (cross-examination).

#### E. Toby in the fisher village
Toby is gutting fish on the dock, his leg bandaged. He is alive because of them.

**What he gives:** testimony that the Cartels passed through the road, threw something into the sea, and threatened his family. **No check.** He is a fisherman, not a scholar, and the court will believe him.

**Bonus:** his family offers the party a **safe house** and a **boat** for Act II.

#### F. The violet flare casing
If they picked it up on the cliff, a **DC 13 Investigation** reveals a maker's mark: a **Vane foundry** stamp. That is dangerous knowledge. Presenting it in court could damage Seraphel — and the party is not ready for that fight. **DM note:** let them choose whether to use it. If they do, Seraphel's offer changes.

**Cross-examination:** Renn will challenge each witness. The party can object (**DC 13 Persuasion/Insight**) or let it ride.

### 5.5 The trial — skill challenge

**Goal:** **3 successes before 3 failures.** The trial has five phases; the party chooses who speaks and what they argue.

| Phase | Approach | Check | Success | Failure |
|-------|----------|-------|---------|---------|
| **1. Opening** | Legal argument | DC 14 Persuasion or DC 13 History/Law | The court listens; +1 success | Renn cuts them off; +1 failure |
| **2. Evidence** | Present the Codex and the ledger | DC 13 Investigation or DC 14 History | The Codex is entered as evidence; +1 success | The Codex is impounded early; +1 failure |
| **3. Witnesses** | Call Helena, Tamsin, Arlen, or the Breaker | DC 13 Persuasion or DC 12 Insight | A witness lands; +1 success | A witness is discredited; +1 failure |
| **4. Cross** | Challenge Renn's Cartel witness | DC 14 Deception or DC 13 Intimidation | The witness cracks; +1 success | The party looks like bullies; +1 failure |
| **5. Closing** | Sum up | DC 14 Persuasion or DC 13 Performance | The court is moved; +1 success | The court is bored; +1 failure |

**Modifiers:**

- **Helena's log:** advantage on Phase 1.
- **The Cartel ledger:** +1 automatic success in Phase 2 (once).
- **Arlen's testimony:** advantage on Phase 3.
- **Tamsin's testimony:** +1 automatic success in Phase 3, but Renn may call him a criminal.
- **The captured Breaker:** +1 automatic success in Phase 3, if the party promised to speak for him.
- **Toby:** +1 automatic success in Phase 5, if rescued.
- **Bribe (200 gp):** automatic success in one phase, but note **Censor's Shroud** for later. Do not tick it in Act I.
- **Public gallery:** if the party has been generous or famous, **DC 12 Persuasion** to sway the crowd; success gives advantage on Phase 5.

**Outcomes:**

- **3+ successes:** the court drops the criminal charges. The Codex is released to the party under a **scholarly writ** (or to Helena's custody, if they prefer). Fine reduced to **50 gp**.
- **2 successes:** the court imposes a **200 gp fine** and impounds the Codex for "authentication." Seraphel can release it later, with conditions.
- **1 or fewer:** the court finds against them. **500 gp fine** or **impressment**. Seraphel intervenes before sentencing, but the party owes him a significant favour.

### 5.6 The Cartel witness

Renn calls a **Cartel witness** — a well-dressed factor named **Marek Voss** (no relation to Helena; the coincidence is deliberate and awful). He claims the party attacked his salvage crew and stole a "family heirloom."

**What he is:** a Cartel front man, coached by Rorik. He is smooth, plausible, and lying.

**How to break him:**

- **DC 14 Insight:** his story has a hole — he claims the Codex is a family heirloom, but it is bound in pre-Silence oak and has civic marks.
- **DC 14 Investigation:** the Cartel ledger lists the Codex as "pending," not as a heirloom.
- **DC 13 Intimidation:** he sweats and looks at the back of the court.
- **Kael** (if alive): he can identify Marek as Rorik's man. This is the cleanest win, but it costs Kael his safety.

**If Marek is not broken:** he damages the party's credibility. **+1 failure.**

### 5.7 Trial mini-game — objections, jury, and gallery

If your table enjoys courtroom play, add these layers. They are optional; the skill challenge works without them.

#### Objections
Once per phase, a player can **object** to Renn's ruling or the Cartel witness's testimony.

- **DC 13 Persuasion/Insight:** the objection is sustained; the party gets **advantage** on the next check in that phase.
- **Failure:** the objection is overruled; the party takes **−1** on the next check.
- **Abuse:** if a player objects more than once per phase, Renn warns them. A second overruled objection adds **+1 failure**.

#### The jury
Port Meridian uses a **panel of three magistrates**, not a jury: **Renn**, a **harbour magistrate**, and a **scholarly assessor** from the Exchange. Each has a different priority.

| Magistrate | Cares about | How to win them |
|------------|-------------|-----------------|
| **Ilya Renn** | Procedure and precedent | Legal argument, clean evidence, no theatrics |
| **Harbour Magistrate Cray** | Order and trade | Show the Cartels are bad for business; mention the lost cargo |
| **Assessor Halden** | Provenance and scholarship | Present the Codex correctly; do not exaggerate |

**DM note:** Osk, the Admiralty clerk, is not on the panel — he is the Cartel contact. If the party exposes him during the hearing, the court turns against the Cartels. If they do not, his paperwork quietly favours them. The captured Breaker's testimony is the cleanest way to expose him.

#### The gallery
The Gilded Docks court has a public gallery: dockhands, scholars, Cartel sympathisers, and one man in townhouse livery.

- **DC 12 Persuasion/Performance** to play to the gallery; success gives **advantage on Phase 5**.
- **DC 13 Insight** to spot the man in livery; he is taking notes. `[DM-ONLY: Seraphel's agent. Do not confirm.]`
- If the party accuses the Cartels publicly, the gallery reacts. A strong speech can **+1 success**; a weak one can **+1 failure**.

#### Evidence chain
The court cares about **chain of custody**. For each piece of evidence, ask: *"Who found it, where, and who has held it since?"*

- If the party can answer clearly: **advantage** on the phase where they present it.
- If the chain is muddled (e.g., the Codex was carried by three PCs, dropped in the sea, and dried on a pub table): **disadvantage**.
- **Helena's log** fixes the chain for the Codex, if she testifies.

#### The verdict table (optional)
Use this finer-grained table instead of the outcomes in §5.5 if you want the result to reflect every phase:

| Successes | Verdict | Consequence |
|-----------|---------|-------------|
| **5** | Acquittal and a scholarly writ | Codex released to the party; fine waived; Seraphel is impressed |
| **4** | Acquittal with conditions | Codex released under Helena's custody; 50 gp fine |
| **3** | Reduced charge | 200 gp fine; Codex impounded for authentication; Seraphel can release it |
| **2** | Conviction | 500 gp fine or impressment; Seraphel intervenes before sentencing |
| **0–1** | Maximum penalty | Impressment; Seraphel pays the fine and takes the Codex; the party owes him everything |

### 5.8 The verdict

Before Renn can rule, the doors open. **Seraphel arrives.**

Do not resolve the verdict fully. Let the tension hang, then let the patron walk in.

>

### 5.10 Legal arguments and their risks

If the players want to build a case, use this table. Each argument is a real position with a real risk. The court is not stupid; it has heard them all before.

| Argument | Check | Strength | Risk |
|----------|-------|----------|------|
| **"The charter covers the site, not just the copper."** | DC 14 History/Law | Strong on precedent; the charter is vague | Renn can read the writ narrowly; **−1** if the party cannot cite a case |
| **"We acted to prevent a collapse."** | DC 13 Persuasion + Helena's log | Sympathetic; the site was unsafe | The court may say the party caused the collapse |
| **"The Codex is a public good."** | DC 14 History/Arcana | Appeals to the scholarly assessor | Renn calls it sentiment; the Cartels call it theft |
| **"The Cartels were looting, not salvaging."** | DC 14 Investigation + the ledger | Damning, if the chain of custody holds | The Cartels have a witness who says otherwise |
| **"The Breaker in the cells will testify."** | DC 13 Persuasion + a deal | Corroborates the Cartel route | His word is cheap; Renn may discount it |
| **"The Exchange ordered the suppression."** | DC 14 Persuasion + Arlen | Exposes the real crime | Arlen risks his career; the Exchange retaliates |
| **"We are owed a finder's fee, not a fine."** | DC 13 Persuasion | Practical; the court understands money | It concedes the Codex is an antiquity; the fine may stand |
| **"The gold man is the real thief."** | DC 15 Insight/Investigation | True, and dangerous | No proof; the court may see it as a conspiracy theory |

**Rule:** a good argument can win a phase even if the roll fails. Reward the player's reasoning with **advantage** or a **lower DC**. The dice are there to create tension, not to overrule good play.

**If the party lies:** **DC 14 Deception** against Renn's Insight. On a success, they gain a phase; on a failure, they lose one and Renn warns them about perjury.

### 5.11 Alternate verdicts and their consequences

The trial should matter beyond a fine. Use this table to carry the result into Act II.

| Verdict | Immediate consequence | Act II consequence |

## 6. Seraphel's Entrance (30 min)

### 6.1 The arrival

> *The doors open without a sound. A man in midnight-blue and silver walks in as if the court were a room he had built. He is not tall, but the room rearranges itself around him. He looks at the magistrate, then at you, and smiles like a teacher who has found his students arguing over the wrong question.*

**Lord Seraphel** is polite, warm, and entirely in control. He does not raise his voice. He does not threaten. He pays the clearance tax in full, cites the **Concord of Meridian** salvage precedent, and asks the court to release the Codex into **scholarly custody**.

**What he says:**

> *"The past does not belong to the magistrates, my friends. It belongs to anyone with the courage to bring a lamp into the dark."*
>
> *"I have read the preliminary catalogue. It is not cult debris. It is a city's memory. I will sponsor its authentication personally."*
>
> *"Consider my purse at your disposal."*

### 6.2 What he wants

- **The Codex** — not to keep, but to **sponsor authentication**. He asks to take it to New Ilya, under the party's name.
- **The transit map** — he asks precise, technical questions about the lines, the nodes, and the alloys.
- **The party** — he wants them close, grateful, and useful.

**His questions:**

- *"The lines in the mosaic — were they raised or inlaid? Did they warm when the rings turned?"*
- *"The star-glass — did it sing, or only shine?"*
- *"Who else has seen the Codex? Be precise."*

**DM note:** he logs every answer. He is not asking out of curiosity. He is building a technical picture of the Engine. **Do not reveal this.**

### 6.3 The offer

Seraphel offers:

- **Full fellowship to the Grand Lyceum, New Ilya** — letters of introduction, a stipend of **100 gp each**, and a patron's protection.
- **Travel with his retinue** — safe passage, a carriage, and a chance to study the Codex on the road.
- **A place in the authentication** — the party will be credited as discoverers, not just labour.

**In exchange, he asks:**

- The Codex travels with his retinue.
- The party shares their findings with his scholars.
- They do not sell or copy the transit map without his knowledge.

**If they refuse:** he accepts gracefully. *"Of course. Scholarship is not a leash."* He still offers the fellowship, but the stipend is smaller and the protection is thinner. Act II is harder, not impossible.

**If they negotiate:** **DC 14 Persuasion** gets them **150 gp each** and the right to keep a copy of the Codex. **DC 16** gets them a **written guarantee** that the Codex remains in public custody. He admires a good negotiator.

**If they accuse him:** he is not offended. He is interested. *"What do you think I am?"* He answers every question honestly, from a certain point of view, and leaves them with more doubt than they started with.

### 6.4 The truth `[DM-ONLY]`

- Seraphel is **not** saving them out of kindness. He is acquiring them.
- He already knows about the Star Chamber. His agents have been watching since the Scriptorium.
- He wants the Codex because it is a **technical document**, not a historical one. The transit map is a schematic.
- He does not want the ancient gods to return. He wants to become a god himself.
- **Do not reveal any of this.** Play the warm, brilliant patron. The betrayal lands in Act IV.

### 6.5 The party's choice

Ask the table directly:

- *"Do you accept the fellowship?"*
- *"Do you let him take the Codex?"*
- *"What do you tell him about the mosaic?"*

**Record the answers.** They shape Act II.

### 6.6 Seraphel conversation tree

Use this if the table wants to talk to him at length. He is the most important NPC in Act I; give him room.

**If they ask about the Codex:**
> *"A city's memory. Hospital rolls, grain accounts, the names of midwives. The kind of thing that makes a magistrate uncomfortable and a scholar weep. I want it authenticated, not owned."*

**If they ask why he helped them:**
> *"Because you brought a lamp into the dark and did not run. That is rarer than talent."*

**If they ask about the Cartels:**
> *"Rorik is a shopkeeper. He sells what he finds. The question is who taught him that history has a price."* (He does not answer.)

**If they ask about the gold man:**
> *"A rumour. Gold attracts rumours."* (He smiles.) *"If you meet him, do tell me. I collect interesting people."*

**If they ask about the mosaic:**
> *"The lines are the important part. Not the nodes — the lines. A map is a picture; a line is a promise."* (He logs their answer.)

**If they accuse him:**
> *"Of what? Saving you from a fine? Funding your scholarship? I am a patron, not a saint. You may dislike me and still take my money."*

**If they refuse the fellowship:**
> *"Of course. Scholarship is not a leash."* (He means it, and that is what makes him dangerous.)

**If they ask what he wants:**
> *"A world where no child weeps in the dark because the granary is empty. Is that so terrible?"*

**Tell:** he never raises his voice. He never lies outright. He answers every question with a truth that serves him. **Do not play him as a villain. Play him as the most reasonable man in the room.**

### 6.7 What Seraphel offers each PC

Seraphel is a good reader of people. He makes a personal offer to each PC, quietly, as if it were a favour between friends.

| PC | What he notices | What he offers | What it costs |
|----|-----------------|----------------|---------------|
| **Dan (Warforged Twilight Cleric)** | The coat, the quiet, the way he stands between danger and others | *"You carry a light in a dark place. I can give you a larger congregation."* A chapel in New Ilya, a stipend, and a chance to preach | The chapel is in Vane's gift; his sermons will be heard |
| **Zook (Rock Gnome Artificer)** | The copper, the tinkering, the hunger to build | *"Your hands are wasted on salvage. I have a workshop that would make you weep."* Access to Vane foundries and pre-Silence alloys | The workshop's work is not entirely his own |
| **John Fantasy (Changeling Warlock)** | The mask, the charm, the secret | *"You are a person of many faces. I collect people who can be more than one thing."* A place at court, a patron's protection, an introduction to a circle of Ascendant scholars | He wants to know what John Fantasy's patron wants |
| **Bog (Bugbear Monk)** | The stillness, the strength, the discipline | *"You have made a temple of your body. I can give you a mountain to train on."* A monastery stay, a teacher, a title | The teacher is Vane's man; the title binds him |

**How to use this:** after the group offer, Seraphel finds each PC alone for a moment. Do not roll dice. Let the player decide. Record what they accept.

**If a PC refuses:** he is not offended. *"A person who knows their own mind is worth more than a person who agrees with me."* He means it, and that is what makes him dangerous.

**If a PC accepts:** he is delighted, and the cost is invisible until Act IV.

## 8. Level 4 and the Act II Hook (15 min)

### 8.1 Level 4

**Trigger:** survive the Inquest and accept (or outmanoeuvre) Seraphel's patronage.

**What each PC gains:** an **Ability Score Improvement or feat**, plus their class features for level 4. Housekeeping is not tracked at this table — each player updates their own sheet.

**Downtime for the level-up:** describe it on the road or in the city. Ask each player: *"What does your PC want now that they have a future?"*

### 8.2 The hook

Seraphel's carriage is waiting at the **Gilded Docks** at dawn. The road to **New Ilya** takes **six days**. The Codex is packed, the letters are signed, and the city is already telling the story of the party who went under the sea.

**Read-aloud:**

> *The carriage is black lacquer and silver, with the Vane falcon on the door. The driver is polite. The horses are better than anything in Port Meridian. Behind you, the city is waking up. Ahead, the road climbs inland, toward a university that has been waiting a thousand years for someone to ask the right question.*

**The final image:** a **rider** gallops up behind them, carrying a sealed letter. The seal is the **Grand Lyceum's**, not Seraphel's. The letter says:

> *"Your fellowship is noted. The Council of Deans requests your presence at the earliest opportunity. Bring the Codex. The Council will hear scholars, not sponsors."*

**End the chapter.** Do not answer who sent it. That is Act II.

### 8.3 The road to New Ilya — optional travel montage

If you have time left, run the first day of the journey. It is a bridge, not a new chapter; keep it to **15–20 minutes**.

**The six-day road:**

| Day | Landmark | Scene |
|-----|----------|-------|
| **1** | **The South Gate** | The city falls away; Seraphel's driver is polite and silent; a first-night camp |
| **2** | **The Broken Bridge** | A pre-Silence span, half-collapsed; the carriage must go around; a chance to explore |
| **3** | **The Waystation** | A Vane-run inn; good food, soft beds, and a locked room the party is not invited into |
| **4** | **The Mire** | A flooded lowland; bandits or a Cartel patrol; the carriage is vulnerable |
| **5** | **The Ridge** | A view of New Ilya's white towers; Seraphel asks a question about the Codex |
| **6** | **The Gates** | The Lyceum's gates; the city is larger, colder, and full of scholars |

**Travel scenes (choose one per day):**

- **A conversation with Seraphel** — he rides with them for an hour, asks about their homes, and remembers every answer.
- **A conversation with the driver** — a Vane servant who has seen things and says less than he knows.
- **A night watch** — a Cartel scout, a soul-light, or a harmless fox.
- **A roadside shrine** — a chance for Dan to pray, or for John Fantasy to leave an offering.
- **A letter from Arlen** — if they wrote to him, a reply arrives at the waystation: *"Do not trust the patron. Do not tell him I said so."*
- **A dream** — use the dreams table from Session 4.

**Do not** run a full encounter unless the table wants one. The point is to show the party that the world is bigger than Port Meridian, and that Seraphel's reach is long.

| **They refuse the fellowship** | He accepts gracefully. They can still go to New Ilya independently, with less money and more danger. |
| **They accuse Seraphel in court** | He is not offended. He answers honestly enough to sow doubt, and the court rules in his favour. The party loses face but keeps the Codex. |
| **They attack Seraphel** | He does not fight. He leaves, wounded and polite. The fellowship is withdrawn. Act II becomes harder, not impossible. |
| **They bribe the court** | Automatic success in one phase, but note **Censor's Shroud** for later. The bribe is remembered. |
| **They break Marek** | The Cartel case collapses. Rorik blames them personally. **+1 Cartel Interference**, but the trial is easier. |
| **They fail the trial** | Seraphel intervenes before sentencing. The party owes him a significant favour; the Codex is impounded. |
| **Tamsin testifies** | He is compelling but fragile. Renn calls him a criminal. If the party defends him, +1 success. |
| **Arlen testifies** | He is precise and damning. If the party mocked him earlier, he refuses; they must find another witness. |
| **The captured Breaker testifies** | He confirms the Cartel orders. If the party kept their promise, he is calm; if not, he recants. |
| **They run long** | Cut the shopping and one downtime scene. Keep the trial and Seraphel. |
| **They run short** | Add a Cartel ambush at the docks, a full downtime scene per PC, and a second witness cross-examination. |
