## 2. Recap and the Breath at the Door (10 min)

Open on the door.

> *Round brass, green with age, open a hand's width. The air through it is dry and cold and smells of ink. The hiss goes on and on — and under it, if you are very still, there is a rhythm. Slow. Wet. Not machinery.*

**Ask the table:**
- *"Who is closest to the door?"*
- *"Is anyone still holding the ledger leaf?"*
- *"What did you do with Tamsin?"* (or *"Did you save the Breaker?"*)

**If Tamsin was saved in Session 2**, he is up top in a ward, or wrapped in a blanket at the warehouse if they brought him. He is not the hiss. **Wren** is.

**If Tamsin was killed or left**, the hiss is still Wren. The party has simply had less warning about what the Breakers are.

**Listen at the door** — **DC 12 Perception**:
- Success: *"That's breathing. Fast, shallow, and getting slower."*
- Failure: they hear only the hiss. The rhythm becomes clear once the door is open.

**Do not** reveal Wren's name, condition, or allegiance until the door is open.

## 3. Opening the Brass Door (30 min)

### 3.1 The mechanism

The door is a **pneumatic pressure seal**, not a lock. It has no keyhole; it has a brass dog-clamp and a rubber gasket gone hard with age.

**To open it:**
- **DC 14 Strength** — haul the clamp and the door together. **Crowbar: advantage.**
- **DC 13 Thieves' Tools** — walk the clamp free without tearing the gasket.
- **DC 12 Investigation** to spot the **snapped Cartel pick** jammed in the seam. If spotted, the party gets **advantage** on either check — they can use the existing tension instead of fighting it.
- **Arlen** can help with a lever, but he is not strong. **Aid Another** only after someone else has started.

**Failure:** the door does not move. The gasket groans. **Failure by 5 or more** triggers the chapter's consequence.

### 3.2 Failure by 5+ — choose one

1. **The stirges.** The pressure spike wakes **2 stirges** roosting in the ceiling seam of 1.2. They come through the dust toward the warm bodies. Short fight, 2 rounds, annoying rather than lethal.
2. **The seal tears.** The gasket splits. The door opens, but the Scriptorium's dry air floods out and the room begins to **sweat** — ink runs, parchment curls, and any unprotected paper in the room is ruined in 10 minutes. This raises the stakes for the Oath rubbing and the schematic.
3. **The Breakers hear it.** If you are using the scouts in Beat 5, they arrive **early**, at the door, while the party is still crowded around it.

Do **not** use all three. Pick the one that fits the table's energy.

### 3.3 Wren

When the door gives, a body falls out.

> *A man in a Breaker's oilskin, grey-faced, lips cracked, eyes open and not seeing. He is breathing in short wet pulls. He has been in there long enough that his lamp is dry and his water skin is a folded rag.*

**Wren** is a Breaker, early twenties, and he has been trapped for **four days**. He was inside when the door re-sealed — the pick snapped, the pressure equalised, and Rorik's crew left him rather than spend another hour on the clamp.

**If Tamsin Roke survived Session 2**, Wren is his survey partner — the man Tamsin thought was dead. That reunion is worth playing. If Tamsin died, Wren does not know yet, and someone will have to tell him.

**Stabilise him:** DC 10 Medicine, a healer's kit, *Spare the Dying*, or *Cure Wounds*. He is at **0 HP** and stable-but-dying; without help he dies in about an hour.

**If saved**, he talks in pieces. See §13 and §7 (Option A).

**If left**, he dies while they explore. The hiss stops. **That silence should be noticeable.** If they come back and find him dead, do not moralise — just describe the quiet.

**If they kill him**, tick **Cartel Interference** twice. Helena finds out.

### 3.4 What the door tells them

- **DC 13 Investigation:** the clamp was forced recently — fresh gouges, modern steel, no corrosion.
- **DC 12 History/Religion:** the door's outer face carries a small civic seal — a set of scales over a sheaf. Not a cult mark.
- **Arlen**, quietly: *"They sealed a reading room. Not a treasury. A reading room."*

## 4. The Scriptorium — Exploration (40 min)

The room is **40 ft by 30 ft**, with a 15-ft coffered ceiling and a raised desk at the far end. It is **dry, cold, and dustless** — the only room down here without two feet of dust. That should unsettle them.

> *A dry reading room. Sloped desks in two ranks. Ink-pots still stoppered. A vow cut deep above the desk, the letters sharp enough to cast shadows. Nothing has moved in here for a very long time.*

### 4.1 The room's rule `[DM-ONLY]`

The Scriptorium is **preserved**. Paper, ink, and leather are intact — until the door is opened and outside air gets in. Once the party has been inside for **10 minutes**, the room begins to react:

- **Ink** in open pots skins over.
- **Parchment** on the desks curls at the edges.
- **The Oath rubbing** must be taken within the first 10 minutes, or it smudges (see §5.2).

**Tell them this through Arlen**, not as a rule: *"Get the rubbings now. The air is changing."* It creates urgency without a timer they can argue with.

### 4.2 The Scriptorium key

| Area | What's there | Checks / payoffs |
|------|--------------|------------------|
| **A. The sloped desks** | Two ranks of reading desks, each with a stone frame and a single leaf left open. Some are notes, some are accounts, one is a child's drawing of a bird. | **DC 12 Investigation:** the desks are *occupied* — the room was abandoned mid-shift, not cleared. **DC 13 History:** the dates in the margins are all within the same week, c. 1,100 BS. |
| **B. The vow inscription** | Cut deep above the raised desk, in High Elonian. The letters are sharp; the stone is undamaged. | **The Oath.** See §5. Do not translate it yourself — Arlen does. |
| **C. The document cabinets** | Six brass-fronted cabinets along the north wall. Five are sealed. One has been **forced** — the lock drilled, the door hanging. | **DC 12 Investigation:** the forced cabinet is empty except for a few vellum scraps and a modern **steel drill bit**. The Cartels have already been here. **DC 13:** they took loose leaves and small relics, not the heavy copper. |
| **D. The pneumatic tube station** | A brass manifold with six capsules in a rack, and a speaking-tube mouthpiece. | **DC 12 Tinker's Tools/Investigation:** the system is still pressurised. One capsule is **loaded and unread**. See handout, §16. A second capsule is a mundane supply requisition (grain, lamp oil, bandages). |
| **E. The healer's cache** | A low cabinet with a red wax seal, broken open by the Breakers but mostly intact. | **2× Potion of Healing**, a healer's kit with **8 uses**, a bone scalpel, and a folded linen bandage still sterile. Canon treasure. |
| **F. The scribe's cell** | A curtained alcove behind the desk: a cot, a bowl, a dry inkstone, and a personal diary. | **No check.** The diary is humanising, not a clue: *"Third day of the fever. Grain enough for nine. The ward wants twelve. I have written to the Exchange and heard nothing."* Let a player read it aloud. |
| **G. The salvage pile** | Copper pipe, brass fittings, a broken astrolabe frame, and a coil of wire, stacked neatly by the door. | **120 gp certified salvage.** Heavy — about 60 lb. See §6 and §8. |
| **H. The raised desk** | The Index Tablet sits on the desk, not in a cabinet. Beside it, a rolled schematic. | **The Tablet** and **the schematic.** See §6. **DC 14 Investigation:** the desk has a hidden drawer (the Tablet was *meant* to be hidden). The drawer is empty — someone knew. |
| **I. The floor** | The flagstones near the door are **newly cracked**, with a fine dust sifting down from the joints. | **DC 13 Investigation/Stonework:** the floor is undermined. **Do not explain why.** This is the collapse in Beat 6. |

### 4.3 Exploration prompts

If the table is quiet, use these:

- **The child's drawing.** A bird with too-long wings, labelled in a child's hand. **DC 12 History:** the bird is a **grey tern**, extinct for 800 years. Do not explain why a child in a sealed archive drew one.
- **The ink.** One pot is still **wet**. It should not be. Say nothing. Let them test it.
- **The desks.** Every leaf is face-up, as if the readers expected to come back.
- **The silence.** No vermin. No dust. The only sound is Wren breathing, if he is alive.

**Arlen's arc in this room:** he starts delighted, then recognises the forced cabinet, then reads the Oath, then breaks. Do not rush him to the confession. Let the room do the work.

## 5. The Oath (40 min, including Arlen)

### 5.1 Reading it

The inscription is **High Elonian**, cut deep and unweathered. Arlen can translate it, but not quickly.

- **Base time: 30 minutes** of uninterrupted work.
- **DC 13 History (or Religion) aid** from a PC who can read the old civic script, or who has relevant background: reduces the time to **20 minutes** and gives Arlen advantage on any check to catch a nuance.
- **If the room is sweating** (door failure §3.2, option 2), the rubbing must be taken in the first 10 minutes or it smudges. Arlen can still read the stone.

**No check to read it aloud.** Once Arlen has the time, he reads it. The roll is for the aid, not for whether the Oath exists.

### 5.2 The text — read this aloud, in full

> *"Not to usurp the heavens, but to ensure mortal children walk without weeping when the hearth grows cold.*
> *We keep account, we mend bone, we measure star — that no prayer need be spent for bread.*
> *Let the ledger be honest, the ward be open, and the door be shut only against the dark."*

**Then Arlen stops.** Let the table sit with it.

> *"That's not a cult. That's a *civic oath*. That's a city promising to feed its own sick."* (beat) *"They were *us*."*

### 5.3 The effect `[ESTABLISHED CANON — Mystery 1, Stage 3]`

This **confirms** the humanist thesis. Say it plainly, once:

- The Ascensionists were **reformers**, not death-cultists.
- They built hospitals, kept grain accounts, measured stars for irrigation and quarantine.
- The "death cult" story is **someone else's version**, written later.

**Do not** explain who wrote the other version, what the Engine is, or why the city fell. That is Acts II–IV.

**If a PC is a cleric or religious:** the Oath's language is familiar — it echoes a Myrraenic hospital vow, or a Selyran measurement prayer. It was not heresy. It was **orthodoxy once**.

**If a PC is an artificer or mason:** the "measure star" line is not poetry. The desk has a sighting groove cut into it, aligned with a shaft in the ceiling. They were doing **practical astronomy** in here.

### 5.4 Arlen's confession — Clue C `[ESTABLISHED CANON]`

This is the scene the whole chapter has been building toward. Stage it.

**Setup:** Helena is at the door with Wren (or up top). Arlen is alone at the desk with the Oath. The party is between him and the exit. He does not run.

**Trigger:** the party shows him the ledger leaf from Session 2, **or** asks him directly why the Exchange calls this cult debris, **or** simply lets him finish reading the Oath. Any of the three.

> *"The Exchange masters told me to log this as cult debris and sell the vellum by weight."* (beat) *"I wouldn't. I wrote 'ceremonial' and put it in the back room. That's why they sent me out here with a salvage crew. To make sure I didn't make trouble."*

Then, quieter:

> *"There's a backup copy. At the Brass Astrolabe. The masters keep a folio of everything they've pulled out of the Warrens and didn't declare. If you want to know what they've been hiding, it's there."*

**This is Clue C.** It is the **social vector** for the chapter's central revelation, and it is also the first crack in Arlen's loyalty.

**Do not** have him confess everything. He does not know about the Engine, Seraphel, or the Cartels' buyer. He knows the Exchange is **lying by omission** and he is complicit.

**If the party threatens him:** he tells them anyway, then goes quiet. He is not brave, but he is not a liar by choice.

**If the party comforts him:** he cries, briefly, and then gets angry — at the masters, not at them. That anger is useful in Chapter 2.

**If they never trigger it:** he confesses on the walk out, when the Oath rubbing is safe in his satchel. Do not let the clue be missable.

**Fallback (chapter canon):** if Arlen is dead or absent, the Cartel fence offers a stolen Scriptorium leaf next morning for 80 gp. It carries the same confession in a margin note.

### 6.2 The Catalogue Index Tablet

> *A slab of black stone the size of a folio, veined with copper. Concentric rings are inlaid in silver — seven of them — and fine conduit lines run from the rings to the edges. It is warm. It should not be warm.*

**What it is (DM):** the index to the Star Chamber. It is the plot key to Chapter 2. **Do not explain the rings, the warmth, or the conduits.**

**What the party can learn tonight:**
- **DC 13 Arcana/Investigation:** it is a **key**, not a book. The rings rotate.
- **DC 15 History:** the ring pattern matches the ceiling shaft in the Scriptorium — it was made for this building.
- **Arlen** (no check): *"It's an index. It tells you where the *real* catalogue is. And it's not here."*
- **Anyone who casts *Detect Magic*:** abjuration and divination, old, layered. **No school they recognise as a whole.**

**If they try to sell it:** Sella Orr will not touch it. A Cartel fence offers **150 gp** and asks no questions — and then reports them. Do not let it leave their hands without a scene.

### 6.3 The schematic

> *A rolled sheet of vellum, still supple. Concentric rings, and lines running out from them like veins. At the centre, a small circle, unlabelled. In the margin, one line of High Elonian: "The door remembers."*

**This is an Engine foreshadow.** `[DM-ONLY]` Do not explain it. Do not name the Engine. If a player asks what it is, Arlen says: *"I don't know. It's not a building. It's a *plan* of one."*

**It is the same phrase Arlen decodes from the Tablet overnight** (*"When Selyra's Eye opens, the door remembers"*) — but tonight he only has *"the door remembers."* Let the players connect it in Chapter 2.

## 7. Complication (25 min)

Pick **one**. This beat exists to make the exit cost something and to put a face on the Cartels before Chapter 2.

### Option A — The Breakers (faction pressure, recommended)

Two **Breaker scouts** come down the shaft, looking for Wren and the snapped pick. They are not a strike team; they are a recovery detail, and they are **not eager to die**.

**Staging:** they arrive at the cellar mouth while the party is in the Scriptorium. A lantern bobs in the 1.2 dust. One calls out: *"Wren! You in there? Rorik wants the clamp."*

**What they want:**
- Wren, alive or dead.
- The pick, or proof it snapped.
- Anything small and valuable the party will part with.

**What they know (if pressed):**
- Rorik's crew surveyed 1.3 three weeks ago and took "the loose stuff."
- They were paid by a **"gold man"** in Ilya coin for "star-glass and pipe-metal." `[DM-ONLY: Seraphel's cutout. Never confirm.]`
- They are **terrified of the brass door** and will not go past it.

**Resolution paths:**
- **Parley (DC 13 Persuasion/Intimidation):** they take Wren (if the party gives him up) or leave empty-handed. Tick **Cartel Interference** once for the parley, per the chapter's rules.
- **Fight:** use **Bandit** stats (§14). They fight for two rounds, then one runs. If both die, tick **Cartel Interference** twice.
- **Trickery (DC 14 Deception):** convince them the room is sealed and Wren is dead. They leave. No tick.
- **Bribe (25 gp):** they take it and go. No tick, but they report the party's faces.

**If the party gives them Wren:** he screams. Let that land. Helena will hear about it.

### Option B — The stirges (attrition, short)

The pressure change wakes **3 stirges** in the ceiling coffers. They drop on the rear rank while the party is reading or looting. Two rounds, 2–3 HP each, but their blood drain is nasty at level 1. Use this if the table needs a quick fight and the session is running long.

### Option C — The ghoul returns (if it fled in Session 2)

The wounded ghoul has followed them through the dust, desperate and stupid. It attacks from the doorway while they are at the desk. **HP 8**, no paralysis (it is too weak), flees at 3 HP. It is a mercy kill or a chase, not a set-piece.

## 8. Collapse and Escape (20 min)

### 8.1 The trigger

The floor gives way **as they leave** — after the Tablet is in hand, after the Oath is read, after the complication is resolved. Not before. It is the chapter's closing door, not a punishment.

**Pick the cause that fits your table:**
- **Cartel powder** (if the Breakers were there and lost).
- **Shifting fault** (if the party never met the Breakers).
- **Leave it ambiguous.** The chapter says to. Do not answer the question.

### 8.2 The collapse

> *The flagstones behind you drop. Not one — all of them, in a single sheet, like a held breath let go. Dust comes up in a wall. The brass door swings shut on its own weight and the hiss stops.*

**Resolve it fast:**
- **DC 13 Dex save** for anyone still in the room: **2d6 bludgeoning** and they are **prone** on the edge. Success: half damage, prone, still holding what they were carrying.
- **Anyone carrying the copper** (60 lb) has **disadvantage** on the save unless they drop it. Make them choose. If they drop it, it is gone — 120 gp in the dark.
- **The Tablet** is 8 lb and does not impede them. If they leave it on the desk, it is gone — and Chapter 2 stalls. **Do not let that happen silently:** Arlen screams *"The tablet!"* as the floor goes. Give them one free grab if they are within reach.
- **Wren**, if alive and with them, must be carried or he dies. A PC can carry him at half speed.

### 8.3 The escape

The shaft is still open, but the cellar floor around it is cracking. **Three rounds** to get everyone up the rope, or a skill challenge:

- **3 successes before 2 failures** — DC 12 Athletics (climb), DC 12 Investigation (find a safe anchor), DC 13 Acrobatics (cross the cracking flagstones), DC 10 Medicine (get Wren up without killing him).
- **Failure:** the rope anchor pulls, and the last person up takes **1d6** and loses one item (their choice).

**Helena is at the top.** She has been listening to the collapse. Her first question is not about the treasure.

> *"Where's Arlen?"*

**If Arlen is alive:** he comes up last, satchel clutched to his chest, and immediately lies about what is in it.

**If Arlen is dead:** Helena goes very still. That is a Chapter 2 problem, and it should hurt.

## 9. Surface — Certification and the Law (25 min)

This is the session's social climax, and the campaign's central legal trap. Run it properly.

### 9.1 At the warehouse

Helena takes inventory in the rain, by lantern, and writes down everything — including the Tablet.

> *"That's black stone with silver in it. That's not salvage. That's an antiquity, and you know what the writ says."* (beat) *"I'll give you a minute to decide what you're telling me."*

**She is not bluffing, and she is not turning them in.** She is giving them a chance to be honest with her. What she does next depends on their answer:

- **If they declare it:** she logs it, and the party gets a **finder's fee** (25 gp) but the Admiralty impounds the Tablet. Chapter 2 then requires them to petition for access, or steal it back, or let Seraphel "sponsor authentication" — all viable.
- **If they hide it:** she writes *"black stone, uncertified"* and says nothing more. The party keeps the Tablet, but the lie sits between them and Helena. Tick **Censor's Shroud**? **No** — global clocks do not tick in Act I. Instead, note it: **Helena remembers.**
- **If they lie badly (DC 12 Deception vs her Insight +5):** she knows, and says only: *"Don't make me choose between you and my licence."*

### 9.2 At the Brass Astrolabe

Sella Orr `[NEW]` certifies the copper in twenty minutes. She does not ask where the rest came from.

- **120 gp salvage**, minus Helena's tenth (**12 gp**), split four ways: **27 gp each** on top of wages. (Plus the 35 gp old coinage if they kept it.)
- **She will not certify the Tablet.** She looks at it for a long time and then says: *"Where did you get this?"* — and does not wait for an answer. She writes a receipt for the copper only.
- **If Arlen confessed in §5.4**, he is visibly uncomfortable in this room. Sella notices. **DC 13 Insight:** she knows something is wrong with him.
- **If the party mentions the backup folio**, Sella goes pale and closes the shutter. *"Not here. Not tonight."* That is Chapter 2 material.

### 9.3 The watcher `[DM-ONLY]`

As they leave the Exchange, a man in a townhouse's livery is standing under the awning across the street, out of the rain. He is not hiding. He watches them come out, looks at each face in turn, and writes something in a small book.

**If approached:** he is polite, says he is "counting deliveries," and leaves. **DC 14 Insight:** he was counting *them*.

**Do not name Seraphel. Do not name the townhouse.** This is the first thread of Act I's patron, and it should be a splinter, not a reveal.

## 11. Level 2 and the Chapter 2 Hook (10 min)

### 11.1 Level 2

**Trigger:** clear the Scriptorium **and** recover the Catalogue Index Tablet. (If they leave the Tablet, they still clear the room but do not level until they recover it — make that explicit in the fiction, not as a punishment.)

**What each PC gains (2024 rules):**
- **HP:** roll or take average, + Constitution modifier.
- **Dan (Twilight Cleric 2):** *Channel Divinity* (Twilight Sanctuary, Turn Undead), one more spell slot, one more prepared spell.
- **Zook (Artificer 2):** *Infusions* (4 known, 2 active), *Replicate Magic Item*, one more spell slot.
- **Kit (Warlock 2):** *Eldritch Invocations* (2), one more spell slot, one more known spell.
- **Bog (Monk 2):** *Ki* (2 points), *Unarmored Movement* (+10 ft), *Flurry of Blows*, *Patient Defense*, *Step of the Wind*.
