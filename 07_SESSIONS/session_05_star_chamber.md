## 3. The Sea Cave (25 min)

### 3.1 The place

The cave is **60 ft wide, 40 ft deep**, with a **shingle floor**, a **tidal pool** at the back, and a **brass-and-basalt door** in the north wall. The ceiling is lost in darkness. The tide is low but coming in.

**Read-aloud:**

> *The cave breathes. Every wave pushes a line of foam across the shingle and then pulls it back with a sound like a held breath. The brass door is green with age, half-open, and warm to the touch. Beyond it, the dark is dry.*

### 3.2 The sentry

A **Cartel Scout** is posted just inside the door, bored and cold. He has a lantern, a crossbow, and a flask.

**Options:**

- **Sneak past (DC 13 Stealth):** the party reaches the door without alerting him. If they take him out quietly (DC 14 Stealth + an attack, or DC 13 Deception to send him away), the Cartels inside are unaware.
- **Talk (DC 13 Persuasion/Deception):** he thinks they are the relief crew. He complains about Rorik, the tide, and the "big man." He can be bribed (10 gp) or persuaded to leave (DC 15).
- **Fight:** he is a Scout (CR 1/2). He shouts if he can; if he shouts, the Cartels inside prepare (see §6.6).
- **Avoid:** there is a **seaward entrance** at low tide (DC 14 Athletics to climb the rocks) that bypasses the sentry but leads to the tidal pool and the reef-crawlers.

**What the sentry knows:**

- *"Kael's inside with two men. They've been stripping the room for two days. The plinth's still shut."*
- *"The book's cursed. One of the lads touched it and his shadow went wrong."*
- *"Rorik's coming at dawn. He wants the glass, not the book."*
- *"There's a way out through the flooded shaft, but not at high tide."*

### 3.3 Cave points of interest

| # | Feature | Check | Payoff |
|---|---------|-------|--------|
| 1 | **Tidal pool** | DC 12 Perception | Something glints under the water: a **star-glass shard** (25 gp) and a **drowned Cartel boot** |
| 2 | **Old shrine niche** | DC 13 Religion/History | A pre-Silence **tide-prayer** to Selyra; the party can leave an offering for advantage on one navigation check |
| 3 | **Cartel cache** | DC 13 Investigation | A waterproof satchel: 2 **healing potions**, 20 gp, a **signal whistle**, and a **note from Rorik** (handout §12.4) |
| 4 | **Shingle bank** | DC 12 Survival | Tracks: four men, a mule, and something dragged. The Cartels are inside. |
| 5 | **The brass door** | DC 13 Investigation | Fresh pry marks; the door was forced within the last day. The mechanism is damaged but usable. |
| 6 | **Seaward crack** | DC 14 Athletics | A narrow climb to a second entrance; leads to the tidal pool and a **reef-crawler** (optional) |

### 3.4 Cave activities

The sea cave is more than a corridor. Give the players something to investigate before they open the door.

| Activity | Check | Result |
|----------|-------|--------|
| **Read the tide-line** | DC 12 Survival | Predict the next surge; advantage on the tide clock |
| **Search the shingle** | DC 13 Investigation | A drowned Cartel satchel: 10 gp, a dry tinderbox, a **signal whistle** |
| **Listen at the door** | DC 12 Perception | Two voices; one says "the glass," the other says "Rorik." |
| **Study the door** | DC 13 Investigation | The mechanism is damaged; it can be jammed open (DC 12 Thieves' Tools) to keep their exit clear |
| **Speak to the sea** | DC 13 Religion/Arcana | A Selyran tide-prayer; a PC can ask for a sign. The tide answers with a single cold wave. No mechanics. |
| **Fish** | DC 12 Survival | A small catch; fresh food and a moment of calm |
| **Mark the exit** | No check | Chalk, rope, or a lantern; advantage on the escape in §7 |
| **Collect star-glass** | DC 14 Investigation | A shard in the pool (25 gp); cold to the touch |

### 3.5 The tide clock

The tide rises in **2 hours**. Track it openly. If the party spends too long in the cave or the chamber, the seaward exit floods and the only way out is the Cartel shaft — which is guarded.

**Tide stages:**

1. **Low:** safe. The seaward crack is passable.
2. **Rising:** the pool connects to the sea; the crack is difficult terrain; **DC 12 Athletics** to use it.
3. **High:** the crack floods; the cave floor is knee-deep; the brass door is the only dry exit. The Cartels' shaft is the only other way out.

Do not drown them. The tide is a pressure clock, not a death sentence.

## 4. The Antechamber of Measures (35 min)

### 4.1 The place

A low vaulted room, **30 ft by 20 ft**, with **six stone benches**, **measuring rods**, **clay tags**, and a **wall of shallow niches**. The air is dry. A **bronze door** at the far end is closed but not locked.

**Read-aloud:**

> *A room built for work. Stone benches in two ranks, each with a groove worn into the edge. Rods of bronze and bone, graduated like rulers. Clay tags on cords, each with a number and a date. Someone measured things here, carefully, for a very long time.*

### 4.2 Points of interest

| # | Feature | Check | Payoff |
|---|---------|-------|--------|
| 1 | **The benches** | DC 12 Investigation | The grooves are **sighting lines**; the room was used for practical astronomy |
| 2 | **The measuring rods** | DC 13 History/Arcana | Calibration tools, not weapons; one rod is **silver-inlaid** (25 gp) |
| 3 | **The clay tags** | DC 12 Investigation | Intake dates, ward numbers, grain weights; a **filing system** |
| 4 | **The niches** | DC 13 Investigation | Most are looted; one still holds a **star-glass lens** (25 gp) and a **scroll of Lesser Restoration** |
| 5 | **The calibration chart** | DC 14 History | A wall chart of star positions; the **Eye, Helm, and Plough** are circled in red |
| 6 | **The Cartel bedrolls** | No check | Three bedrolls, a cold lamp, a half-eaten meal, and a **ledger** with Cartel accounts |
| 7 | **The bronze door** | DC 12 Investigation | Not locked; the Cartels left it open. Beyond: the Star Chamber. |

### 4.3 The trap

The floor between the benches is a **pressure plate** — old, but still armed. The Cartels avoided it; their bootprints go around it.

**Spot:** DC 14 Perception (or DC 12 if moving at half speed).
**Disarm:** DC 13 Thieves' Tools or DC 14 Investigation (jam the mechanism).
**Trigger:** a **counterweight** drops a stone slab from the ceiling. **DC 13 Dex save**, **2d6 bludgeoning**, and the slab blocks the door for **1 round** (DC 15 Athletics to lift).

**If the party triggers it:** the noise carries into the Star Chamber. Kael sends the Scout to investigate. This is a complication, not a disaster.

### 4.4 The Cartel ledger

The ledger is a **merchant's account book** with entries like:

> *"Two crates star-glass — 240 gp. One black stone — 400 gp, pending. Books — burn. Scholar — do not touch. Payment: Vane mark, triple rate."*

**DC 14 Insight:** the handwriting is not Rorik's. The "Vane mark" is a falcon and calipers. `[DM-ONLY: Seraphel's cutout. Do not confirm.]`

**If they take the ledger:** it is evidence for the Inquest in Session 6. It can also be sold to Sella for 50 gp — but she will burn it.

### 4.5 Optional: the returning scout

If the party made noise or triggered the trap, the Scout from the cave enters the antechamber. He can be ambushed, bribed, or talked down. If he escapes, he warns Kael.

### 4.6 Antechamber activities

The room rewards curiosity. Use these to make the exploration feel like **research**, not a search check.

| Activity | Check | Result |
|----------|-------|--------|
| **Copy the calibration chart** | DC 13 History or Cartographer's Tools | A valuable copy; +1 success at the Inquest in Session 6 |
| **Read the clay tags** | DC 12 Investigation | Names, ward numbers, grain weights; a humanising detail |
| **Test the measuring rods** | DC 12 Arcana or Tinker's Tools | The rods are calibrated to the dome; advantage on the puzzle |
| **Map the room** | DC 12 Investigation | A sketch; advantage on the collapse escape |
| **Search the niches** | DC 13 Investigation | The lens and the scroll; a **hidden compartment** with 30 gp in old coin |
| **Study the Cartel ledger** | DC 13 Investigation | The "Vane mark" entry; evidence for the Inquest |
| **Repair the bronze door** | DC 13 Tinker's Tools | The door closes quietly behind them; +1 on the first Stealth check in the Star Chamber |
| **Set a tripwire** | DC 14 Stealth or Thieves' Tools | Advantage on the first round against the Cartels, if they come through the door |
| **Rest** | Short rest | Costs 1 hour; the tide rises one stage |

**DM note:** every activity that produces a physical object (a copy, a map, a sketch) should be noted on the party's inventory. Those objects matter in Session 6.

## 5. The Star Chamber (45 min)

### 5.1 The place

A **circular chamber, 50 ft across**, with a **dome of lapis and silver wire**. The constellations are inlaid in **cold silver**, and they **move** — slowly, almost imperceptibly. At the centre, a **stone plinth** holds a **book bound in salt-swollen oak**. Three **silver rings** are set into the floor around the plinth. The floor is a **mosaic map**.

**Read-aloud:**

> *The dome is a sky made of stone. Constellations burn cold overhead — not painted, but inlaid in silver wire, and moving. The floor is a map. At the centre, on a plinth of black stone, a book waits. It is dustless. The room has been holding its breath.*

### 5.2 The room key

| Area | What's there | Checks / payoffs |
|------|--------------|------------------|
| **A. The dome** | Constellations in silver wire; three are brighter: the **Eye**, the **Helm**, the **Plough** | DC 13 Arcana/History: these are Selyran star-signs, not gods |
| **B. The three rings** | Silver rings set into the floor, each with eight star-signs; they rotate | DC 12 Investigation to understand; see §5.3 |
| **C. The plinth** | Black stone, warm; the Codex on top; a **silver seam** around the base | DC 14 Investigation: the seam is a lock; the rings are the key |
| **D. The floor mosaic** | A map of Port Meridian and five inland nodes, joined by copper lines | DC 13 Investigation/Navigator's Tools: it is a **transit map**, not a trade map |
| **E. The wall niches** | Six niches, mostly empty; one holds a **star-glass lens** and a **clay tag** | DC 12 Investigation: the Cartels missed it |
| **F. The dry fountain** | A conduit hub, dry, with copper veins running into the floor | DC 14 Arcana: the room is **connected** to something deeper; do not explain |
| **G. The Cartel camp** | Three bedrolls, a lantern, a pry bar, and a **half-packed crate** of star-glass | Loot: 2 star-glass shards, 40 gp, a **crowbar** |
| **H. The collapsed shaft** | A rough hole in the south wall, shored with modern timber | The Cartels' entrance; **DC 13 Athletics** to climb; leads up to Warehouse Nine |
| **I. The hidden compartment** | Under the plinth, a **stone drawer** with a **star-chart cylinder** | DC 15 Investigation; the cylinder is a Chapter 3 hook, not a Chapter 2 answer |

### 5.3 Selyra's Eye — the puzzle

The three rings must be aligned to match the Tablet:

- **The Eye** at the top.
- **The Helm** to the left.
- **The Plough** to the right.

**How to solve:**

1. **Read the Tablet:** it shows the alignment. If Arlen's note is in hand, this is automatic.
2. **Read the dome:** the three bright signs match the rings. **DC 13 Arcana/History** identifies them.
3. **Read the antechamber chart:** the circled signs confirm the alignment. **DC 12 Investigation** if they searched the room.
4. **Use the star-glass lens:** it is **not a key** — canon is explicit that it has no key function — but focusing it on the dome throws the three bright signs into sharp relief. **Advantage on the next Arcana/History check** to identify the alignment. It does not turn a ring.

**Mechanics:**

- Rotating a ring takes **one action**. A PC can rotate one ring per turn.
- **Wrong alignment:** the ring locks and **1d8 radiant damage** arcs through the handler. The dome grinds. **Three wrong alignments** alert the Cartels (§6.6).
- **Correct alignment:** the ring clicks; a constellation brightens. All three correct: the plinth opens with a **deep click**, dust falls, and the Codex is accessible.
- **Forcing the plinth:** **DC 18 Athletics**. Success: the plinth opens, but the Codex takes **1d4 damage** (one excerpt is torn; choose one). Failure by 5+: the plinth cracks and the room begins to collapse early.
- **Knock:** the plinth is not a door. The spell fails, but it reveals the seam and grants **advantage** on the next Investigation check.
- **Dispel Magic:** the rings are not magical in a way the spell can unpick. It suppresses the backlash for **1 round**, allowing one free rotation.

**Hints if the table is stuck:**

- Tamsin: *"Wren said the Eye goes at the top. He was always right about the sky."*
- The antechamber chart is circled in red.
- The dome's three bright signs pulse when a ring is close.
- A PC with the **Sailor** or **Sage** background recognises the star-signs.

**Do not** let the puzzle kill the session. If they are stuck after 10 minutes, let the lens grant advantage again, or have Tamsin remember Wren's note.

### 5.4 The Catalogue Codex

The Codex is a **book bound in salt-swollen oak**, 25 lb, with **brass corner-pieces** and **vellum pages** that are still supple. It is warm. It should not be.

**What it contains (read selected excerpts aloud):**

#### Excerpt 1 — Hospital intake
> *"Fourth Ward. Intake: 37. Discharged: 21. Died: 9. Remaining: 7. No soul turned away. No fee recorded for the destitute. The ward is not a prison."*

#### Excerpt 2 — Quarantine law
> *"The sick are not to be chained. The door is to be shut only against the dark, not against the dying. A physician who abandons a patient is no physician."*

#### Excerpt 3 — Midwife rota
> *"The child is entered in the roll before the mother is thanked. The roll is the city's memory. If the mother dies, the child is not nameless."*

#### Excerpt 4 — Grain account
> *"Nine measures for twelve wards. Write nine. Do not write twelve and hope. A false account is a second famine."*

#### Excerpt 5 — Aqueduct gradient
> *"The gradient is measured by the star, not the eye. The star does not lie. The eye is a servant."*

#### Excerpt 6 — Margin note
> *"If the gods close the granary, we will build our own."*

**What this changes `[ESTABLISHED CANON — Mystery 1, Act I → Act II]:`** the Oath already **confirmed** that the Ascensionists were civic humanists. The Codex does not confirm it again. It **recontextualises** that humanism at continental scale: hospitals, grain accounts, aqueducts, and star-measured nodes joined into one system. This is the *"answer generates two questions"* beat. **Do not** explain who suppressed it or why.

**If a PC is a cleric:** the language echoes a Myrraenic hospital vow. It was **orthodoxy once**.
**If a PC is an artificer or mason:** the aqueduct and star-measurement notes are practical engineering, not poetry.
**If a PC is a warlock:** John Fantasy's patron is silent, but the room feels **watched** — not hostile, just observed.

### 5.5 The transit map

The floor mosaic shows **Port Meridian** as a bright node, joined by **copper-veined lines** to **five dark nodes inland**. One line is **scratched out** — deliberately, recently, with a tool.

**What the party can learn:**

- **DC 11 Navigator's Tools or Intelligence:** the lines are not roads; they are **conduits**.
- **DC 13 History:** the map predates the city by centuries; Port Meridian was built on top of an older node.
- **DC 14 Investigation:** the scratched-out line once ran **north**, toward New Ilya.
- **DC 15 Arcana:** the map is **warm** in the same places the Tablet is warm. It is a circuit, not a picture.

**Helena's line (if she is present, or via a PC):** *"That's not a trade map. That's a machine plan."*

**Do not name the Engine.** Do not explain the nodes. Let the players draw their own conclusions.

### 5.6 Optional: the star-glass steward

If the party **forces the plinth** or **damages the dome**, a **Star-Glass Steward** (CR 3 construct) activates in the wall niche. It is not hostile unless the Codex is removed by force.

> **Canon check:** this is a lesser chamber-construct. It is **not** the CR 7 Star-Glass Warden of Nythara in `08_MONSTERS_AND_ITEMS/monsters_and_foes.md`. Keep the names distinct and do not imply a shared origin yet.

The Cartels have stripped the loose glass but have not forced the plinth or damaged the dome. That is why the Steward has not woken for them.

> *A figure unfolds from the wall: a slender construct of dark glass and silver wire, its face a smooth lens. It raises one hand and says, in High Elonian: "Account for what you take."*

**Resolution:**

- **Show the Tablet (DC 10):** it recognises the index and stands down.
- **Speak the Oath (DC 12 History/Religion):** it bows and opens the plinth.
- **Fight:** use the **Star-Glass Steward** block (§14). It is a hard fight, not a lethal one; it stops at 0 HP and shatters.
- **Ignore it:** it follows the party and repeats the phrase. It does not attack unless they take the Codex without accounting for it.

**Reward for resolving peacefully:** the Steward gives them the **star-chart cylinder** from the hidden compartment. It says: *"The next door is not here."* That is a Chapter 3 hook.

#### D. The old guard post

## 6. The Cartel Breach (45 min)

### 6.1 The arrival

The Cartels breach as the Codex lifts — or earlier, if the party triggered the trap or failed the puzzle three times.

**Kael, Cartel Salvage Captain**, comes through the collapsed shaft with **a Thug and a Scout**; a second Thug follows if the party is reinforced. He is a big man with a hammer, a ledger, and a tired voice.

> *"All right. Nobody's died yet. Let's keep it that way."* (beat) *"The book for four hundred gold. You walk. Rorik never knows you were here. That's a good day for everyone."*

### 6.2 Kael's offer

- **400 gp** for the Codex, paid on the spot from a strongbox.
- **Safe passage** out of the cave.
- **A lie:** he will not let them keep the star-glass or the transit map. He wants the Codex intact, not a copy — and the glass besides.
- **A truth:** he does not want to kill them. He is a professional, not a fanatic.

**If they negotiate:**

- **DC 14 Persuasion:** he goes to **600 gp** and lets them keep the star-glass lens.
- **DC 16 Persuasion:** he admits Rorik is not the real buyer. *"There's a gold man. Pays triple. I don't ask."*
- **DC 13 Intimidation:** he backs off, but the Scout marks their faces. **+1 Cartel Interference.**
- **DC 15 Deception:** convince him the Admiralty is already outside. He panics and withdraws — but takes the strongbox and reports them.

**If they refuse:** Kael sighs, sets the strongbox down, and says: *"I didn't want to do this on an empty stomach."* He topples a pillar. **The collapse begins.**

**If they attack first:** the Scout runs for the shaft; Kael fights; the Thugs try to grab the Codex and run.

### 6.3 The fight

**Default encounter (level 2, hard):**
- **1× Kael, Cartel Salvage Captain** (CR 2) — see §14.
- **1× Cartel Thug** (CR 1/2).
- **1× Cartel Scout** (CR 1/2) — the lookout. He fires once, then runs for the shaft; he does not join the melee.

**Reinforcements:** add a **second Thug** if Tamsin or Toby is fighting alongside the party. If the party is level 3, use the Enforcer variant in §6.3b instead. The goal is a hard fight the party can win or escape, not a stand-and-die.

**If Cartel Interference is 3:** add **2 more Thugs** or a second Scout. If the party has Tamsin and Toby, they can even the odds.

**Terrain:**

- **The plinth:** half cover; the Codex is on it. A PC can grab it as an action.
- **The rings:** difficult terrain; a creature that ends its turn on a ring must make a **DC 12 Dex save** or take **1d4 radiant**.
- **The pillars:** four stone pillars support the dome. Kael can topple one (action, DC 15 Athletics) to start the collapse.
- **The shaft:** the Cartels' entrance; a Thug can escape with the Codex if no one stops him.
- **The mosaic:** slick with dust; **DC 10 Acrobatics** on a dash.

**Tactics:**

- **Kael** opens with a offer, then fights defensively. He targets the PC holding the Codex. He does not pursue into the collapse.
- **Thugs** try to grapple and drag the Codex toward the shaft. They surrender at 10 HP or less.
- **Scout** stays at range, behind a pillar, and flees at half HP.
- **Morale:** if Kael drops below half HP, he offers a truce again. If the Scout dies, the Thugs may surrender.

### 6.3b Cartel crew variants

Adjust the breach to the party's choices and the clock.

| Situation | Crew |
|-----------|------|
| **Default (level 2, fresh)** | Kael + 1 Thug + 1 Scout (lookout; flees) |
| **Cartel Interference 2** | Kael + 1 Thug + 1 Scout, and the Scout has **half cover** behind a pillar |
| **Cartel Interference 3** | Kael + 2 Thugs + 1 Scout, or replace Kael with the **Cartel Enforcer** (CR 4) and add 1 Thug |
| **Party parleyed on the road** | Kael is suspicious; he opens with a deal and does not attack until refused twice |
| **Party killed the road crew** | Kael is angry; he attacks on sight with 2 Thugs, but the Thugs are reluctant |
| **Party rescued Toby** | Toby can raise an alarm or throw a rope; the Cartels are distracted for one round |
| **Party has Tamsin** | Kael recognises him and tries to take him prisoner; Tamsin fights. Add 1 Thug to the default crew |
| **Party is level 3** | Use the **Cartel Enforcer** (CR 4) instead of Kael, plus 1 Thug and 1 Scout |
| **Party is wounded** | Remove the Thug; Kael offers a deal sooner |

**If the fight is too easy:** Kael topples a pillar earlier, and the collapse begins after 2 rounds.
**If the fight is too hard:** Kael offers a truce at half HP, or the Steward intervenes on the party's behalf.

### 6.4 Non-combat resolutions

The fight is not mandatory. Reward creativity:

| Approach | Check | Result |
|----------|-------|--------|
| **Sell a copy** | DC 14 Deception or a real copying effort | Kael takes a partial copy; the party keeps the Codex. He discovers the trick in Session 6. |
| **Split the loot** | No check | Kael takes the star-glass and the strongbox; the party keeps the Codex. He reports them anyway. |
| **Turn Kael against Rorik** | DC 16 Persuasion + a real offer | Kael defects (temporarily). He helps them escape, then disappears. |
| **Bluff the Admiralty** | DC 15 Deception | Kael withdraws. He is not convinced for long. |
| **Use the Steward** | If it is active and friendly | It fights the Cartels, then returns to its niche. It does not leave the chamber. |
| **Collapse the shaft** | DC 15 Athletics or Thieves' Tools | The Cartels are cut off; the party must find another exit. |

### 6.5 Negotiation mini-game

If the party wants to deal with Kael, make it a **three-round negotiation**, not a single roll. Kael has demands; the party has leverage.

**Kael's opening position:**

- He wants the Codex.
- He will pay **400 gp**.
- He will let the party walk.
- He will **not** let them keep the star-glass or the mosaic copy.

**Leverage the party can use:**

| Leverage | Check | Effect |
|----------|-------|--------|
| **The Cartel ledger** | DC 13 Intimidation/Persuasion | Kael realises Rorik is skimming; he is less loyal |
| **The captured Breaker's info** | No check | Kael knows the party has talked to his people; he is wary |
| **The Admiralty patrol** | DC 14 Deception | Kael believes the court is already involved; he wants to avoid a murder charge |
| **The Steward** | No check, if friendly | Kael is visibly unnerved; +2 on the next check |
| **Tamsin** | No check | Kael recognises him; the negotiation becomes personal |
| **A copy of the Codex** | DC 14 Forgery/Calligrapher's Tools | Kael accepts a partial copy; the party keeps the original (he discovers the trick later) |
| **The star-glass lens** | No check | Kael values it at 100 gp; it can sweeten any deal |

**Round 1 — Open:** the party states what they want. **DC 13 Persuasion/Intimidation.**
**Round 2 — Bargain:** the party offers something. **DC 14 Persuasion** (or an automatic success if they offer the lens and 100 gp).
**Round 3 — Close:** the party seals the deal or walks away. **DC 13 Insight** to see if Kael is lying.

**Outcomes:**

- **Three successes:** Kael takes a copy or the star-glass and lets them go. He warns them that Rorik will not be as reasonable.
- **Two successes:** he takes the star-glass and the strongbox, but leaves the Codex.
- **One or fewer:** he attacks, or lets them go and immediately reports them. **+1 Cartel Interference.**

### 6.6 The collapse begins

If Kael topples a pillar, or if the fight lasts **4 rounds**, the chamber begins to fail. Move to §7 immediately. Do not finish the fight as a clean victory; the collapse is the real climax.

## 7. The Collapse (30 min)

### 7.1 The trigger

The dome cracks. Dust falls in sheets. The floor tilts. The sea is coming in through the shaft and the seaward crack.

> *The dome groans. A hairline crack runs from the Eye to the floor, and the silver wire snaps with a sound like a harp string. The mosaic buckles. The plinth tilts. The book is sliding.*

### 7.2 The five-round escape

Track **5 rounds**. Each round, a hazard and a choice.

| Round | Hazard | Save / check | Consequence |
|-------|--------|--------------|-------------|
| **1** | Dust and falling grit | DC 10 Dex | 1d4 bludgeoning; vision obscured |
| **2** | A pillar falls across the exit | DC 13 Dex | 2d6 bludgeoning; the exit is blocked until cleared (DC 14 Athletics) |
| **3** | The floor tilts toward the shaft | DC 13 Acrobatics | Slide 10 ft; drop an item unless held |
| **4** | Sea water surges in | DC 12 Athletics | 1d6 cold; the seaward crack is now the only clean exit |
| **5** | The brass door begins to close | DC 14 Athletics/Acrobatics | Take 1d6 and lose one item if you fail; the door seals behind you |

**What they can carry:**

- **The Codex** (25 lb): requires one free hand or a pack. A PC carrying it has **disadvantage** on Acrobatics checks.
- **The star-glass lens** (1 lb): easy.
- **The pre-Silence coinage** (200 gp, heavy): counts as 20 lb; drop it or slow down.
- **A wounded ally:** Tamsin, Toby, or a surrendered Thug. Carrying them halves speed.
- **A Cartel prisoner:** Kael or a Thug. They know the shaft route; they are also a liability.

**Do not** kill a PC in the collapse without a save. If a PC is trapped, give the others a **DC 13 Athletics** check to free them; failure costs **1d6** and one item, not a death.

### 7.3 The skill challenge alternative

If the table prefers a skill challenge to round-by-round hazards:

**3 successes before 2 failures.** Suggested checks:

- **Athletics** to clear the pillar or lift the door.
- **Acrobatics** to cross the tilting floor.
- **Investigation** to find the seaward crack.
- **Survival** to read the tide and pick the right exit.
- **Medicine** to keep a wounded ally moving.
- **Arcana** to stabilise the dome for one round (a clever, risky option).

**Failure:** the party escapes, but loses one item and takes **2d6** each. The Codex is damaged: one excerpt is lost (choose the least important to the plot).

### 7.4 The sea cave

They emerge into the sea cave as the tide rises. The brass door is half-submerged. The seaward crack is a **DC 14 Athletics** climb or a **DC 13 Acrobatics** dash through the surf.

**If they rescued Toby:** he is at the crack with a rope. He gives them **advantage** on the climb.
**If they brought the mule:** it is on the cliff top, panicking. **DC 12 Animal Handling** to calm it; it can carry the Codex.
**If Kael survived:** he is behind them, shouting. He does not follow into the water. He has a **signal whistle**.

### 7.5 What you carry

The collapse is a choice about **what matters**. Put the following on the table and make the party decide in the moment:

- **The Codex** (25 lb): the chapter's prize. If they leave it, it is lost in the collapse.
- **The star-glass lens** (1 lb): easy to carry and valuable to the right buyer. It has **no key function**, whatever the Vane collectors claim.
- **The mosaic copy / rubbing** (negligible): evidence and a plot item.
- **The Cartel ledger** (1 lb): evidence for the Inquest.
- **The strongbox** (400 gp, heavy): money, but it slows them.
- **The pre-Silence coinage** (200 gp, 20 lb): heavy, valuable, optional.
- **Tamsin** (a wounded ally): he can walk, but slowly. Carrying him halves speed.
- **Toby** (if he came): he knows the cave, but he is not a fighter.
- **A surrendered Cartel** (Kael or a Thug): a prisoner, a witness, a liability.
- **The star-chart cylinder** (negligible): a Chapter 3 hook.

**Ask each player:** *"What are you carrying, and what are you leaving?"* Do not let them carry everything. The collapse is not a packing puzzle; it is a statement about priorities.

**Mechanical effect:**

- Carrying more than **one heavy item** (Codex, strongbox, coinage) imposes **disadvantage** on Acrobatics checks in the collapse.
- Dropping an item is a free action. Picking one up is an action.
- A PC can throw an item to another PC (**DC 12 Athletics**) if they are within 30 ft.

**If they leave the Codex:** describe it sliding into the dark. Do not soften it. The chapter's prize is gone, and Session 6's Inquest becomes a search for another copy.

### 7.6 The cliffhanger

End on the cliff top, wet, exhausted, with the Codex. The tide is in. The Cartels are behind them. And a **signal flare** goes up over the bay — a thin line of violet light. Someone is answering.

**Do not** run the return to Port Meridian. That is Session 6.

**Ask the table:**

- *"What did you leave behind?"*
- *"Who is carrying the Codex?"*
- *"What do you do about Kael?"*
- *"Who saw the flare?"*

### 7.7 Extra encounters in the dark

If the party lingers, explores the side rooms, or takes a short rest, use one of these.

#### A. The Cartel straggler
A wounded **Thug** from Kael's crew is trapped under a fallen beam. He is not a threat. He can be:

- **Rescued:** **DC 13 Athletics** to lift the beam; he answers one question and leaves.
- **Questioned:** **DC 12 Intimidation/Persuasion**; he confirms Rorik's boat is coming at dawn.
- **Ignored:** he dies. No mechanical consequence, but the table will remember.

#### B. The star-glass pool
A pool in the side gallery is full of **star-glass shards**, cold and faintly luminous. **DC 14 Arcana:** the shards are **resonant**; they hum when the rings turn. Taking one is easy; taking a handful wakes a **Star-Glass Steward** (if you have not used it yet).

#### C. The collapsing stair
The seaward stair is failing. **DC 13 Acrobatics** to cross; failure = 1d6 bludgeoning and a dropped item. If the party is fleeing the Cartels, this is a **chase complication**.

#### D. The second brass door
A second brass door, welded shut, set into the wall of a side passage. **DC 13 History/Investigation:** it is a twin of the main door and bears the same pre-Silence ward-marks. An inscription in High Elonian reads: *"Not this door."* The Cartels tried to force it and gave up. **Do not open it in Chapter 2.** It is a hook for later.

#### E. The drowned messenger
A **Preserved body** floats in the flooded gallery, still wearing a **messenger's satchel**. The satchel holds a **sealed letter** addressed to the **Fourth Chair**, never delivered. The letter is an optional, non-canonical hook; cut it if you are not using the Fourth Chair. **DC 13 Religion:** the body has not decayed; the soul has not passed. Do not explain.

### 7.8 Alternate puzzle solutions

If your table hates puzzles, or if the dice turn, use one of these. The chapter must not stall.

| Solution | How | Cost |
|----------|-----|------|
| **The lens** | Focus the star-glass lens on the dome | Advantage on the next ring check; does not turn the ring |
| **The Steward** | Ask it to open the plinth | Requires the Tablet or the Oath |
| **The chart** | Use the antechamber calibration chart | Solves one ring automatically |
| **Brute force** | DC 18 Athletics | Damages the Codex (one excerpt lost) |
| **Knock** | The spell reveals the seam | Advantage on the next check |
| **Dispel Magic** | Suppresses the backlash for 1 round | One free rotation |
| **Tamsin's memory** | He remembers Wren's notes | Solves one ring, but he is shaken |
| **A PC's background** | Sailor, Sage, or Cleric recognises the signs | Advantage on all three checks |
| **The Cartels** | Let Kael's crew solve it for them | They take the Codex; the party must steal it back |

**Rule:** a failed puzzle should change the scene, not end the session. The Cartels arriving early is a complication, not a fail state.

### 7.9 Chase through the sea cave

If the Cartels pursue the party out of the chamber, run the escape as a **chase**. It is fast, loud, and full of choices.

**Zones:**

1. **The Star Chamber** — collapsing; falling stone, tilting floor.
2. **The Antechamber** — the pressure plate, the bronze door, the Cartel bedrolls.
3. **The Sea Cave** — the tide, the shingle, the brass door.
4. **The Seaward Crack** — a narrow climb, slick with spray.
5. **The Cliff Top** — the mule, the road, the weather.

**Chase rules:**

- Each round, every participant makes a **DC 12 Dex (Acrobatics) or Str (Athletics)** check. Success: move one zone. Failure: lose ground; the pursuers close.
- **Complications** (one per zone): a falling pillar (DC 13 Dex), a pressure plate (DC 14 Perception), a tide surge (DC 12 Athletics), a slippery crack (DC 13 Acrobatics), a panicking mule (DC 12 Animal Handling).
- **Stunts:** a player can describe a risky move for **advantage** on the check. If they fail, they take **1d6** and lose an item.
- **Attacks:** a PC can attack instead of moving; they stay in their zone. The Cartels can do the same.
- **The Codex:** a PC carrying it has **disadvantage** on Acrobatics checks unless they hand it off or use a rope.

**Ending the chase:**

- **The party reaches the cliff top first:** they escape, but the Cartels know their faces. **+1 Cartel Interference.**
- **The Cartels catch them:** a short fight, or a deal. Kael wants the Codex, not blood.
- **The party drops the Codex:** the Cartels stop chasing. The chapter's prize is lost.
- **The party cuts the rope / collapses the stair:** the Cartels are delayed. **DC 15 Athletics or Thieves' Tools**; success buys one zone, failure costs one.

### 7.10 The Steward's account

If the Star-Glass Steward is friendly, it can give the party a **lore scene**. It does not explain the Engine, but it tells them what the room was for.

> *"We were made to keep the account. Not the treasure. The account. When the Eye opens, the door remembers. When the door remembers, the city answers. We have been waiting for someone to ask the right question."*

**Questions it can answer:**

- **What is the Codex?** *"The city's memory. The names of the sick, the weight of the grain, the measure of the stars."*
- **What is the mosaic?** *"The veins. Where the city joins itself."*
- **What is the Tablet?** *"The index. It does not open doors. It tells you which door is real."*
- **What happened here?** *"The Eye would not open. The Fourth Chair did not answer. We kept the account until the air changed."*
- **What is the Fourth Chair?** *"A seat at a table that is no longer here."* (It will not say more.)

**DM note:** this is a reward for resolving the room peacefully. It gives the party a genuine understanding of the Archive without naming the Engine. Use it to deepen the mystery, not solve it.

## 8. Level 3 (15 min)

**Trigger:** recover the Codex and survive the collapse. If the Codex is lost or destroyed, they still level if they saw the mosaic and survived — the milestone is **understanding**, not possession.

**What each PC gains:** their class features for level 3. Housekeeping is not tracked at this table — each player updates their own sheet. The milestone is the only thing the DM needs to record.

**Downtime for the level-up:** describe it on the cliff, in the rain, catching their breath. Ask each player: *"What did your PC learn about themselves in the dark?"* Do not run a training montage.

### 8.1 Level-up scenes

If you want the level-up to feel earned, give each PC a short scene on the cliff. No dice, no mechanics — just a moment.

| PC | Scene prompt | What it means |
|----|--------------|---------------|
| **Dan** | He holds the coat of a dead man and the light of a god he cannot see. *"What do you say to Wren?"* | The cleric's faith is tested by a death he could not prevent |
| **Zook** | He counts the copper by lantern light, then stops. *"What is the money for?"* | The artificer decides what he is building toward |
| **John Fantasy** | He looks at the sea and the mask he wears. *"Who are you when no one is watching?"* | The warlock's patron is silent, and that is its own answer |
| **Bog** | He sits still while the others talk. *"What did the dark teach you?"* | The monk finds a centre in the collapse |

**If a player gives a strong answer:** give them **inspiration**. This is the moment for it.

**If they joke:** let them. The game is not a therapy session. But note the joke; it may come back in Act II.

### 8.2 What the Codex changes

The Codex is not just loot. It changes what the party can **do** in Act II. Make that concrete.

| Discovery | What it unlocks |
|-----------|-----------------|
| **Hospital rolls** | The party can argue for public access to the Lyceum's medical archives |
| **Quarantine law** | A legal precedent against the "dangerous knowledge" argument |
| **Grain accounts** | Evidence that the pre-Silence city fed its poor; a political weapon in New Ilya |
| **Star-charts** | A way to read the transit map; the first step toward the Engine |
| **Aqueduct gradients** | A lead on the under-city; useful in Act II's pump station |
| **The Fourth Chair** | Optional hook only; the Lyceum may recognise it — if you choose to keep this thread |
| **The margin note** | The chapter's thesis in one line: *"If the gods close the granary, we will build our own."* |

**If the party copies pages:** they have leverage even if the Codex is impounded. **If they don't:** they are dependent on Seraphel's custody. That is a real choice.

**If they give the Codex to Seraphel:** he is delighted, and the party loses direct access. They can still study it at the Lyceum, but always with his scholars watching.
**If they keep it:** they have the prize, but the Admiralty and the Cartels both want it. Act II starts with pressure.

## 18. Puzzle Handout — Selyra's Eye

Copy this onto a card, or sketch it on the battle mat. Do not give the players the solution; give them the shape.

```
                 THE DOME
        . - ~ ~ ~ - .         Eye
      /    *     *    \        |
     |   *   EYE   *   |     Helm — Plinth — Plough
      \    *     *    /        |
        ` - . _ . - '        floor rings

  RING I   (outer)   : twelve signs, one bright
  RING II  (middle)  : twelve signs, one bright
  RING III (inner)   : twelve signs, one bright

  The Tablet shows three signs in a row.
  The dome shows three signs that are brighter than the rest.
  The floor rings turn.
```

**Player-facing instructions:**

> *Three silver rings are set into the floor around the plinth. Each bears the same twelve star-signs. They turn with a low grinding sound. Above, the dome holds a sky in silver wire. Three stars burn brighter than the others. The Tablet in your hands shows three signs in a line.*

**What the players can try, in plain language:**

- Read the Tablet and match its three signs to the rings.
- Look up at the dome and find the three bright signs.
- Search the antechamber for the calibration chart.
- Use the star-glass lens to sharpen the dome's light (advantage on the check, not a free ring).
- Ask the Steward, if it is awake and friendly.
- Force the plinth, and accept the cost.

**Do not say:** "Roll Investigation to solve the puzzle." Say: *"What are you doing with the rings?"* Then call for a check only if the outcome is uncertain.

**If the table is stuck after ten minutes:**

1. Tamsin remembers Wren's note: *"The Eye goes at the top."*
2. The dome's three bright signs pulse when a ring is close.
3. The lens solves one ring automatically.
4. A PC with Sailor, Sage, or Cleric background recognises the signs.

**If they still cannot solve it:** let them force the plinth. The Codex takes 1d4 damage (one excerpt torn), the Steward may wake, and the Cartels arrive early. The story continues. **Never let a puzzle end the night.**

## 20. The Cartel Crew

Kael's crew are not faceless. If the party talks, surrenders, or takes prisoners, this crew carries the scene. By default only **one Thug** is in the chamber with Kael; the other is on the road or outside. Add the second Thug if the party is reinforced, or if you want the harder version of the fight.

### 20.1 Kael's people

| Name | Role | Wants | Breaking point | What he knows |
|------|------|-------|----------------|---------------|
| **Kael** | Salvage captain | To finish the job and go home | Being called a murderer | The buyer is not Rorik; the "gold man" pays triple |
| **Dennick** | Ledger man | To be indispensable | Being made to look a fool | The payment schedule; the Vane mark |
| **Hask** | Thug, older | His share, and no trouble | A real threat to his family | The route in and out; the tide |
| **Jory** | Thug, young | To impress Kael | Being left behind | Where the sentry is posted |
| **Sisk** | Scout | A bonus and a dry coat | Being cornered | The seaward crack; the Cartel signal whistle |

**Use them:** give each a single line of dialogue. Let the party learn that the Cartels are employees, not fanatics. That makes the fight sadder and the negotiation real.

### 20.2 Reaction table

| Party action | Kael | Dennick | Hask | Jory | Sisk |
|--------------|------|-------|---------|------|------|
| **Offer a bribe** | Interested | Calculating | Wary | Excited | Watching the road |
| **Show the Tablet** | Curious | Alarmed | Bored | Confused | Nervous |
| **Threaten Rorik** | Cold | Frightened | Looks away | Silent | Reaches for a weapon |
| **Offer a copy** | Suspicious | Intrigued | Relieved | Relieved | Doubtful |
| **Mention the Admiralty** | Wants to leave | Starts packing | Panics | Freezes | Bolts |
| **Attack** | Fights defensively | Hides behind the plinth | Fights badly | Follows Kael | Runs for the shaft |

### 20.3 If they take a prisoner

- **Kael** will not betray Rorik, but he will confirm the "gold man" and the payment. He asks for a clean death or a clean cell, not mercy.
- **Dennick** trades information fast and lies about the rest. He is the best source on the Exchange's involvement, and the least reliable.
- **Hask** just wants to go home. He gives the route and the tide table. He has a daughter in the Warrens.
- **Jory** imitates Kael until he is scared, then tells the truth. He knows where Sisk went.
- **Sisk** bargains with the seaward crack and the signal whistle. If released, he reports the party's faces.

**DM note:** a captured Cartel is a witness for Session 6's Inquest. Note who they keep and how they treat him.

### 20.4 If the party kills them all

Do not moralise. Describe the room after. Then have the Steward say, if it is present:

> *"Account for what you take."*

That line lands harder after a massacre. It is not a punishment; it is the chapter's thesis.

### 20.5 Cartel signal codes

If the party searches a Cartel body or the camp, they find a **signal whistle** and a scrap of code:

- **One long note:** all clear.
- **Two short notes:** strangers on the road.
- **Three short notes:** run.
- **One long, one short:** the gold man is here.

`[DM-ONLY: the last signal is never used in Chapter 2. It is a hook for Act II.]`
