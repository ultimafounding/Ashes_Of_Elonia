# How to Use This Bible

This document is the canonical source-of-truth layer for Ashes of
Elonia. It reconciles the existing player guide, Ain Elonar, and DM
campaign material while preserving uncertainty where uncertainty is part
of the setting. It is not yet the session-by-session campaign book.

## Canon hierarchy

| **Priority** | **Authority** | **Rule** |
|----|----|----|
| 1 | Locked canon | Explicit definitive truths override all draft or ambiguous wording. |
| 2 | Ain Elonar | Primary authority for ancient history, mythic narrative, and the Nine, except where intentionally unreliable. |
| 3 | DM campaign material | Authority for campaign secrets and narrative architecture after continuity reconciliation. |
| 4 | Player-facing material | Authority for what modern Elonians commonly know, believe, or repeat. It may be incomplete or mistaken. |

## Information tags

| **Tag** | **Meaning** |
|----|----|
| ESTABLISHED CANON | Objectively true unless deliberately retconned by the DM. |
| DM-ONLY | True, but deliberately concealed from players. |
| PLAYER-FACING | Safe as ordinary modern knowledge. |
| DISPUTED | In-world sources disagree; no version should be presented as settled. |
| UNRESOLVED | Intentionally unanswered. Do not invent a canonical answer. |
| DEVELOPMENT SPACE | Safe territory for later expansion, provided it does not alter foundations. |

## Immutable Canon Register

- The Ascendants are not inherently evil.

- Seraphel is the primary antagonist.

- Seraphel seeks divinity rather than the return of the gods.

- Myrraen is neither villain nor saint.

- Orinth remains hidden as Brother Cael until the intended campaign
  revelation.

- The Soul Crisis originates from damage caused by the Ascension Engine.

- The final chapter of Ain Elonar is blank.

- The campaign’s central theme is freedom versus protection.

- The Avatar of the Last Mercy is certainty made manifest.

# I. Campaign Identity

## Premise

Ashes of Elonia is a mythic fantasy campaign about history, faith,
consequence, freedom, and the danger of certainty. The adventure begins
amid ruins and suppressed records, then expands into a struggle over
who, if anyone, should hold authority over humanity’s future.

## Central question

**If someone protects you from every consequence, are they helping you,
or preventing you from becoming who you could be?**

## Narrative laws

- Prefer mystery over exposition.

- Prefer discovery over explanation.

- Every major revelation answers one question and creates two more.

- No principal ideology receives automatic moral validation.

- Player choice, not divine decree, determines the campaign’s final
  answer.

- Never turn incomplete knowledge into invented foundational cosmology.

# II. Cosmology and Metaphysical Truths

## The Sea Beyond

UNRESOLVED

The Sea Beyond predates the known gods and is described as possibility
rather than a conventional place. The Nine emerged from it, but neither
its origin, nature, purpose, nor ultimate boundaries are defined. This
mystery remains deliberately open.

## The Celestine

ESTABLISHED CANON

The Nine did not create Elonia. They awakened into a world that already
existed and participated in its shaping. Divinity is therefore not
synonymous with primordial creation, but the exact mechanism by which
the Nine became divine is not established and must not be reduced to a
reproducible formula.

## Humanity

ESTABLISHED CANON

Humanity was not created by the Nine. Its origin is unknown. The gods
were fascinated by human mutability: mortals could contradict their
nature, change morally, and disrupt otherwise predictable futures.

## Death and the soulways

DM-ONLY

Varek maintained the passage of souls rather than inventing mortality ex
nihilo as a simple mechanical system. The Ascension Engine damaged the
pathways by which souls travel. That ancient wound later became the Soul
Crisis. What ultimately lies beyond the final passage remains outside
established canon.

## The Veil

ESTABLISHED CANON

The Veil separates Elonia from the realm or state into which most
Celestine withdrew during the Silence. Myrraen’s act severed the
ordinary divine connection to Elonia. The precise metaphysical
construction of the Veil remains intentionally undefined.

# IV. Authoritative History

## Before the First Dawn

The Nine awaken from the Sea Beyond into an unfinished world. They
discover rather than create humanity. Orinth is first to awaken; Myrraen
is last. The origin of both gods and mortals remains unresolved.

## The First Covenant and Age of Revelation

The gods and humanity enter a partnership, not a relationship of
ownership. Divine guidance accelerates law, scholarship, medicine,
agriculture, exploration, culture, and spiritual practice. The Nine
Cities become embodiments of different divine ideals.

## Avarr and the crisis of certainty

Avarr becomes the apparent triumph of the Covenant, then commits
systematic cultural atrocities while convinced of its own moral
obligation. The Celestine stop the atrocities. The event profoundly
destabilises Myrraen’s confidence that prosperity, knowledge, and
protection naturally produce virtue.

## The Ascensionists

The original Ascensionists are reformers, scholars, engineers, and
idealists attempting to reduce dependence on miracles. Their movement is
not inherently evil. Their achievements help lay the foundations for
later mortal arcana and technology.

## The Ascension Engine

An attempt to overcome death escalates into catastrophic
experimentation. Souls are trapped, fractured, and diverted; the
underlying soulways are wounded. The tragedy is driven primarily by
conviction and escalating rationalisation, not simple malice.

## The Last Council and the Silence

The Nine divide over intervention, responsibility, freedom, and
consequence. Myrraen ultimately severs the divine connection and most of
the Celestine depart beyond the Veil. Orinth secretly remains. The
Silence is a radical act intended to force mortal independence, with
catastrophic human costs.

## The World Without Gods

Civilisation initially collapses, then adapts. Mortal medicine, arcana,
governance, engineering, and institutions develop without divine rescue.
Over centuries, the true history of the Divine Age becomes mythologised,
simplified, censored, lost, or deliberately protected.

## The Soul Crisis

Centuries after the Silence, the old damage increasingly manifests as
missing souls, ghosts, spiritual anomalies, and failures of passage. The
crisis is not naturally caused by the gods’ absence. Its root cause is
the Ascension Engine.

## Modern faiths

PLAYER-FACING

Major traditions include the Myrraenic Faith, Penitent Orders, Keepers
of Dawn, and Church of the Last Light. Modern religion is shaped by
interpretation because public divine certainty vanished with the
Silence.

## Player knowledge baseline

- The gods are believed to have departed during the Silence.

- Ancient civilisation exceeded the modern world in many respects.

- Human society survived and rebuilt without overt divine guidance.

- Pre-Silence ruins can be valuable and dangerous.

- Claims about missing dead, awakening ruins, and returning divinity are
  normally treated as rumours or specialist concerns.

# VII. Core Campaign Powers

## The Ascendants

ESTABLISHED CANON

A broad historical and archaeological movement, not an evil cult. Most
members seek recovered truth. Internal layers possess increasingly
dangerous knowledge, but membership alone is not evidence of complicity
in Seraphel’s plan.

## Seraphel, the Hidden Tenth

DM-ONLY

Seraphel is the primary antagonist. Publicly he is a respected
historian, scholar, philanthropist, and prominent Ascendant. Privately
he seeks apotheosis. He does not seek to restore the gods as rulers. He
uses the rhetoric, machinery, scholarship, and expectations surrounding
“return” to create the conditions for his own divinity.

His fatal flaw is certainty: he increasingly treats intellectual
conviction as moral authority. He should remain comprehensible,
charismatic, and often factually insightful, which makes the methods he
chooses more dangerous rather than less.

## Orinth / Brother Cael

DM-ONLY

Orinth alone remained in Elonia after the Silence and now hides as
Brother Cael, caretaker of Lantern Chapel. He is a witness and keeper of
context, not an oracle for player decisions. He should not solve
mysteries the party can solve themselves.

## Myrraen

DM-ONLY

Myrraen’s Silence was neither vindictive punishment nor uncomplicated
salvation. She acted from love, fear, doubt, and a conviction that
divine protection had become harmful. Her choices caused enormous
suffering and also enabled mortal independence. The campaign must
preserve both truths.

## The Avatar of the Last Mercy

DM-ONLY

The Avatar is not simply “evil Myrraen”. It is certainty made manifest:
protective compassion stripped of doubt until prevention of suffering
becomes suppression of choice. It is the thematic extreme of protection
without freedom.

# XIII. Endgame Canon

## Seraphel’s apotheosis attempt

Seraphel uses harvested souls, ancient Ascensionist systems, and a
weakened Veil to attempt divinity. The Return Ritual is not his sincere
attempt to restore the Celestine; it is the metaphysical scaffolding for
becoming the Hidden Tenth.

## The Avatar

The Veil crisis culminates in the manifestation of the Avatar of the
Last Mercy. Its benevolent logic removes agency in the name of
preventing pain. Defeating it is the rejection of absolute certainty,
not a declaration that mercy itself is wrong.

## Varek and the Passing

PROVISIONAL CAMPAIGN CANON

The existing campaign architecture has Varek sacrifice his divine
existence to seal the damaged spiritual passage, becoming the first true
death of a Celestine and reducing the surviving pantheon from Nine to
Eight. This event is retained for the campaign book, but it occurs only
if play reaches the current designed climax; it is not pre-campaign
world-state canon.

## Possible endings

No ending is canon before the players choose it. The established options
are: continued Silence; a permeable Veil and renewed conversation; an
open divine return; or an unprecedented new arrangement created through
player action. These are branches, not predetermined outcomes.

# XIV. Ain Elonar Canon Rules

Ain Elonar is both historical text and campaign object. Its tone may be
mythic and its surviving versions may contain interpretation, but it
preserves essential truths unavailable to most modern scholarship.

- The existing chapters are not permission to explain the Sea Beyond
  completely.

- Later discoveries may recontextualise earlier passages but should not
  invalidate their thematic truth.

- Orinth’s involvement may create marginalia and annotations, but these
  should provoke investigation rather than provide solutions.

- The final chapter, “Of What Comes Next”, remains blank. It must not
  acquire an official canonical ending before the players act.

# XV. Continuity Decisions and Reconciliations

| **Issue** | **Official ruling** |
|----|----|
| Present day listed as c. 1,100 AS, while premise repeatedly says a thousand years after the Silence. | Present day is c. 1,000 AS for Bible v1. The 1,100 AS figure is superseded. |
| Ascendants described in places as seeking the return of the gods. | The movement is pluralistic. Some members may favour return, but Seraphel personally seeks apotheosis and must never be rewritten as sincerely trying to restore the gods. |
| “First Ascendants” language can blur ancient Ascensionists and modern Ascendants. | Ascensionists = ancient self-reliance movement. Ascendants = modern historical movement. The distinction is canonical. |
| Engine described as destroyed yet later “still active”. | The original project was physically broken/abandoned; its metaphysical wound and surviving systems persist. Modern agents can reactivate or exploit those remnants. |
| Myrraen sometimes framed as definitively correct by outcome. | Her hypothesis is never objectively validated. Humanity’s survival is evidence, not proof. |
| Varek’s sacrifice appears as future fact in DM notes. | Treat as planned endgame event, not already-established historical fact; player actions may alter circumstances without predetermining PC choices. |

# XVI. Protected Unknowns

UNRESOLVED

The following questions are deliberately protected from casual
expansion. New material may create evidence, theories, or competing
interpretations, but must not establish a definitive answer without an
explicit future canon decision.

- What the Sea Beyond ultimately is.

- Why the Nine awakened, and whether something caused or intended it.

- The true origin of humanity.

- The final nature or destination of souls beyond their passage.

- Whether Selyra saw any single “true” future.

- Whether Myrraen was ultimately right.

- Whether divine beings are inherently different from mortals at the
  deepest level.

- What is written in the final chapter of Ain Elonar.

# XVII. Development Guardrails for the Full Campaign Book

- Give every mandatory revelation at least three discovery routes.

- Keep Seraphel present and useful before his betrayal.

- Never make ordinary Ascendants guilty by association.

- Do not make Brother Cael solve the party’s problems.

- Let factions hold partial truths rather than arbitrary misinformation.

- Use ruins, records, NPC choices, and consequences as lore delivery
  before exposition.

- Every act should materially change what the players think the campaign
  is about.

- Preserve moral cost in every endgame branch.

- Do not write player-character choices into the plot.

- Keep the final page blank until play supplies the answer.
