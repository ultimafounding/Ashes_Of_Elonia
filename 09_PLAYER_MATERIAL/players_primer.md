# Ashes of Elonia: Player Primer
> *Everything in this handout is common knowledge. Your character grew up knowing most of it.*

## 1. The World You Live In
One thousand years ago, the Nine Celestine — the gods — withdrew from the world. No priest can tell you why; the event is called the Silence, and every faith, university, and grandmother disagrees about what it meant.

What followed is undisputed history. For three hundred years civilization starved: aqueducts built for miracles ran dry, warded granaries failed, and the Age of Ashes killed on a scale the chronicles still won't number. Then mortals did what they do — they rebuilt. Without gods. Engineers raised waterwheels where prayers once sufficed. Physicians learned quarantine instead of invocation. Five powers rose, and for seven centuries the world has been, by most measures, working.

You live in that world: gas lamps and lecture halls, guild contracts and customs inspectors, a continent that runs on law, coin, and ingenuity — built directly on top of a buried age it barely understands.

## 2. The Buried Age
Before the Silence stood the First Concord: taller towers, stranger roads, machines of brass and star-glass whose purposes nobody can reproduce. Its vaults and transit tubes honeycomb the bedrock of every modern city. Digging them up — salvage — is one of the continent's great industries and a standing political problem. Finds belong to whoever holds the charter, the permit, and the muscle, roughly in that order. The black market pays better and asks nothing. That discount always collects.

## 3. The Five Powers
- **The Free Coast.** Independent harbor-cities grown rich on trade and salvage. Loud, pragmatic, lightly governed by an Admiralty Board and bickering guilds. Your home for now: **Port Meridian**, population ~65,000, built on layered ruins it sells by the ounce.
- **The Republic of Ilya.** A state run by its university. The Grand Lyceum at **New Ilya** sets curriculum, currency policy, and what counts as history. If you want a fact certified, you go to Ilya. If you want a fact buried, someone in Ilya already filed it.
- **The Aurelian League.** Sun-plains heartland of legions, highways, and magistrates. Order, duty, solar eagles. Its capital is Solcaris Nova.
- **The Verdancy.** Rain-canopy communes under colossal ironwoods, governed by moots of grovewardens. Timber, medicine, and a standing refusal to be ruled.
- **The Dominion of Vareth.** Desert canyon-cities of black basalt where the dead outnumber the living sixty to one. The Custodians record every birth and death. Their ledgers, lately, will not close.

## 4. Faith Without Answers
The gods are silent and have been for a millennium. What remains:
- The **Myrraenic Faith** and penitent orders, who read the Silence as mercy or punishment depending on the parish.
- The **Keepers of Dawn**, who keep the old rites polished in case the sky ever answers.
- The **Church of the Last Light**, a tiny fellowship that tends candles in forgotten places and asks for nothing.
- University atheism, dockside superstition, and the fringe preachers who say the gods are about to return. Respectable people call that the Taboo of Return and change the subject.

Divine magic exists but it is rare and personal: a cleric's conviction, a paladin's oath, a grandmother's funeral rite that works for reasons no faculty will certify.

## 5. Your Situation
Date: c. 1,000 AS. Place: Port Meridian, the Salt-Lantern Tavern, raining.

**Helena Voss**, licensed salvage factor, is hiring a three-day crew: clear vermin, shore timber, and map a collapsed fault beneath her **Warehouse Nine**. Wages: 15 gold each plus a tenth-share of certified scrap. With you comes **Arlen Marr**, a junior archivist who can read the old script and can't fight at all.

The charter writ covers *salvage* — rope, copper, worked stone. Anything that looks like *antiquities* belongs to the Admiralty unless certified. Unlicensed relics mean fines or naval impressment. The Cartels will offer you a better price in a darker alley. That is your decision to make.

## 6. Making a Character
- **System:** D&D 5th Edition, starting at **level 1**. Advancement is by story milestones.
- **Ability scores:** Standard array or point buy.
- **Peoples:** Humans are the majority everywhere. Halflings, gnomes, dwarves, and tieflings are ordinary citizens of Port Meridian. Anything rarer needs a one-line explanation and the DM's agreement.
- **Classes:** All official options. A few setting notes, not restrictions:
  - *Wizards* graduate from academies; *fighters and rogues* sign on with guilds, customs, or crews like this one.
  - *Clerics and paladins* serve an interpretation of a silent god. No deity answers back. What answers, if anything, is between you and the DM.
  - *Warlocks* hold bargains with something in the dark — ruins, courts, or worse. Define it with the DM; assume your character's theory of what it is could be wrong.
  - *Artificers* are at home in Ilya foundries, League workshops, and Vane-funded laboratories.
- **Alignment:** No evil player characters.
- **Starting gear:** Standard class equipment, plus one salvage tool (crowbar, climber's kit, healer's kit, or scholar's pack).
- **Background hooks (choose or roll 1d4):**
  1. You owe Helena Voss money, a favor, or your life.
  2. The Cartels robbed, hired, or blacklisted you.
  3. You once saw something dead move, and you need to know why.
  4. You want a Lyceum fellowship — the charter is your way in.

## 7. Table Rules
- **Tone:** wonder, dust, and paperwork. Dungeons and archives in equal measure.
- **Hard lines:** sexual violence, harm to children, and torture never appear at this table.
- **X-card:** tap it (or message the DM) and the scene stops or fades to black. No explanation required, no argument permitted.
- **Party rule:** one crew. Keep secrets from each other if you must; never sabotage each other.
