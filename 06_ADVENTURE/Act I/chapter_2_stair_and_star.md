**Chapter question:** *If the ancients were not monsters, who benefits from saying they were?*

## 1. Setup `[PLAYER-FACING]`

The party has the **Catalogue Index Tablet** but the Scriptorium floor collapsed behind them. Warehouse Nine is sealed. The Tablet points to the lower vault: accessible only via the old cliff stairstep from the **Sea Cliff Road**, then back under the bay to the **Star Chamber**.

Helena secures a **two-day overland charter**. Arlen is no longer with the party — he confessed, then left after a petty threat. His translation arrives by courier, grudging and unsigned:

> *"The rings show Selyra's Eye at the top, the Helm to the left, the Plough to the right. The lower door is under the cliff, reached at low tide. The Cartels know. They have been there for two days."*

If the party has no translation, **Sella Orr** at the Brass Astrolabe will decode the Tablet for **50 gp and a favour**, or a PC can attempt it with *Comprehend Languages* plus a **DC 15 History/Arcana** check.

The road is the only way in. The Cartels know it. So does Tamsin.

## 2. DM Truth `[DM-ONLY]`

- The **Star Chamber** holds the **Catalogue Codex** — hospital charters, irrigation law, midwife rotas, grain accounts, and star-charts — plus a **transit-map floor mosaic** hinting at linked conduits continent-wide. **Do not name the Engine.** Describe only *"copper-veined lines joining Port Meridian to inland nodes."*
- A **Cartel strike-team** has beaten the party to the vault via the original Warehouse shaft, with a recovery detail still on the road. They are Breakers, not soldiers: loud, greedy, and willing to talk before they kill.
- **Seraphel** intervenes only at the Inquest, as a benevolent saviour. This is the first move of the campaign's central manipulation. Play him warm, curious, and genuinely useful.
- **Arlen's absence matters.** He is not the villain; he is a proud, frightened man who did the right thing badly. If the party reaches out, he can be recovered. If they don't, he becomes a source of information at the Astrolabe — bitter, useful, and not quite trustworthy.
- **Wren's death matters.** Tamsin is grieving. The Cartels left Wren to die behind a sealed door. That is the first crack in Tamsin's loyalty to Rorik.
- **The captured Breaker** in magistrates' custody is a live thread. He can identify the Cartel route, Rorik's paymasters, and the "gold man" — but only if the party thinks to ask.
- **The precious stone** Arlen threw back was dark, heavy, and valuable enough that the Cartels made him get rid of it. It might be **star-glass**; it might be something else. **Leave it undefined for now.** It is buried in the collapse, and someone may try to dig it out. That is a Chapter 3 problem, not a Chapter 2 answer.
- **The Cartels' "glass" is not Arlen's stone.** In the Star Chamber the Cartels are buying and stealing **star-glass** — shards, lenses, and raw stock. Their client pays for the glass first and the Codex second. Do not let the table collapse the two mysteries into one unless you decide to.

## 5. Part A — The Sea Cliff Road

### 5.1 The route

The Sea Cliff Road runs **six miles** south of Port Meridian along the old basalt shelf, then turns inland through a collapsed sea arch and down a stair cut into the cliff. It is old, narrow, and not maintained. The wind is constant. The drop is real.

**Travel rules:**

- **Normal pace:** 6 miles in about 4 hours. **Fast pace:** 3 hours, but the party takes a **−2 penalty** on Perception and arrives tired (one level of exhaustion unless they rest).
- **Weather:** roll or choose each leg. **1–2 calm, 3–4 gusting, 5 rain, 6 storm.** Gusts impose disadvantage on ranged attacks and climbing checks. Rain erases tracks and makes the basalt slick. Storm is a hazard, not a cutscene.
- **Tides:** the low-tide pool and the sea cave are only passable at low tide. The party has roughly **four hours** of safe access. Track it openly; the pressure is part of the road.

### 5.2 Waypoints

| # | Waypoint | Type | Purpose |
|---|----------|------|---------|
| 1 | **The Tide Cut** | Exploration / hazard | Show the road is old and the sea is hungry |
| 2 | **The Wind Stair** | Skill challenge | Make the cliff feel earned |
| 3 | **The Wrecked Mule** | Social / evidence | Cartel presence; the "gold man" clue |
| 4 | **The Reef-Crawler Nest** | Combat | Attrition; a nest with something in it |
| 5 | **The Drowned Shrine** | Lore / Soul Crisis | The dead are not passing; no explanation |
| 6 | **The Star Stair** | Arrival / cliffhanger | The Cartels are already here |

### 5.3 Clues on the road

- **Cartel survey chits** with the split-coin mark.
- **New Ilya oilskin** and violet-white lamp oil.
- **A gold coin** stamped with the Vane falcon and calipers. `[DM-ONLY: Seraphel's cutout money. Do not confirm.]`
- **Wren's route-mark** — a Breaker chalk-sign that Tamsin recognises. It leads toward the Star Stair, not away.
- **A drowned sailor's shade** at the low-tide pool, flickering violet, shadow detached. **DC 12 Arcana:** *"the passage stutters."* No cure at this tier.

### 5.4 The Cartel presence

The Cartels have a **recovery detail** on the road: one **Cartel Scout** and two **Thugs**, plus a mule that has gone lame. They are not expecting the party. They can be:

- **Parleyed with** (DC 13 Persuasion/Intimidation): they give up the route and the "gold man" rumour, but report the party's faces.
- **Ambushed** (DC 13 Stealth): the party gets a surprise round.
- **Bribed** (25 gp): they leave, but the Cartels learn the party is on the road.
- **Fought:** use the blocks in the session file. One flees at half HP.

### 5.5 The night camp

The road has one safe ledge: a dry overhang above the tide line, with old fire-scarring and a broken shrine-niche. The party can camp here. Use the night for:

- **Tamsin's story** — what Rorik is like, what Wren was like, why he stayed.
- **Watch rotations** — a Cartel lantern on the road below, a reef-crawler in the water, or a harmless pod of seals (a false alarm).
- **Dream / omen** — Dan's aura, John Fantasy's patron, Bog's stillness, Zook's copper. Keep it atmospheric, not mechanical.

## 6. Part B — The Star Chamber

### 6.1 Approach

The Star Stair descends from the cliff road through a collapsed sea arch into a **sea cave** at low tide. The cave floor is wet basalt and barnacle crust. At the back, a **brass-and-basalt door** — the lower entrance to the Star Chamber — stands half-open. Cartel pry marks are fresh.

**Hazards:** slick footing (DC 10 Acrobatics), a surge channel (DC 12 Athletics or 1d6 cold + swept 10 ft), and a nest of **reef-crawlers** in the tidal pool.

### 6.2 The Antechamber of Measures

A low vaulted room with **six stone benches**, **measuring rods**, **clay tags**, and a **wall of shallow niches**. The niches have been looted — by the Cartels, recently. One niche still holds a **star-glass lens** and a **clay tag** in High Elonian: *"For the Eye, when it opens."*

**Clue:** the antechamber is a **calibration room**, not a tomb. The rods are graduated; the tags record measurements. **DC 13 History/Investigation:** this is applied science, not worship.

### 6.3 Selyra's Eye — the puzzle

The Star Chamber dome is inlaid with **lapis and silver wire** in a constellation map. Three **rotatable silver rings** are set into the floor around the plinth. Each ring bears **star-signs**. The Tablet shows the alignment: **the Eye-star + the Helm + the Plough**.

**Mechanics:**

- **DC 12 Investigation** to understand the rings rotate.
- **DC 13 Arcana/History** to identify the three signs on the Tablet and match them to the dome.
- Each wrong alignment: **1d8 radiant backlash** and a grinding noise that carries through the stone. **Three wrong alignments** alert the Cartels (they arrive early).
- Correct alignment: the plinth unlocks with an audible *click*; dust falls; the Codex is accessible.

**Alternate solutions:** a PC can force the plinth (**DC 18 Athletics**, damaging the Codex on a failure), use *Knock* (the plinth is not a door; the spell fails, but reveals the mechanism), or trace the conduit lines with **DC 15 Investigation** to bypass one ring. Reward cleverness; do not gate the chapter on one roll.

### 6.4 The Catalogue Codex

A book bound in **salt-swollen oak**, dustless, heavy. It contains:

- Hospital intake ledgers and quarantine law.
- Midwife rotas and grain accounts.
- Star-measured aqueduct gradients.
- A margin note: *"If the gods close the granary, we will build our own."*

**This is not a second confirmation.** The Oath already confirmed the humanist thesis in Chapter 1. The Codex **recontextualises** it at continental scale — hospitals, grain, aqueducts, and star-measured nodes as one system. It is the *"answer generates two questions"* beat. Let the players read selected pages; do not summarize the whole book.

### 6.5 The transit map

The floor mosaic shows **Port Meridian** as a bright node, joined by **copper-veined lines** to **five dark nodes inland**. Helena (DC 11 Navigator's Tools) says: *"That's not a trade map. That's a machine plan."*

**Do not name the Engine.** Do not explain the nodes. Let the players draw their own conclusions.

### 6.6 The Cartel strike-team

As the Codex lifts, the Cartels breach from the old shaft:

- **1× Kael, Cartel Salvage Captain (CR 2)** — use the block in `session_05_star_chamber.md`; if the party is level 3 or reinforced, upgrade to the **Cartel Enforcer (CR 4)** in `05_FACTIONS/the_archive_cartels.md`.
- **1× Thug (CR 1/2)**.
- **1× Scout (CR 1/2)** on the ledge with a crossbow — the lookout, who fires once and flees.

Kael offers once: *"Book for 400 gold and walk."* If refused, he topples a pillar. The chamber begins to collapse in **5 rounds**. The fight is winnable, but the clock is the real threat. If Tamsin or Toby is fighting alongside the party, add a **second Thug**; if the party is level 3, use the Enforcer instead of Kael.

### 6.7 Collapse and escape

The chamber gives way. The party must choose what to carry:

- **The Codex** (25 lb, plot artifact) — needs protection; water and falling stone can ruin it.
- **The star-glass lens** (25 gp, cold to the touch).
- **200 gp pre-Silence coinage** (heavy).
- **A wounded ally** (Tamsin, if he came).

**Escape:** a **skill challenge** (3 successes before 2 failures) using Athletics, Acrobatics, Investigation, or Survival, or a **5-round dash** with DC 12 Dex saves. Failure costs HP and an item, not the chapter.

## 7. Part C — The Gilded Docks Inquest

### 7.1 The charges

Admiralty magistrates impound the Codex under **salvage-vs-antiquities law**. Magistrate **Ilya Renn** charges the party with **unlicensed antiquarianism** — fine **500 gp** or **impressment** into the naval salvage corps.

**The legal reality:** the Codex is an antiquity, not salvage. Helena's charter covers copper and fittings, not books. The party needs a defence, a patron, or a deal.

### 7.2 The skill challenge

**Goal:** avoid impressment and keep the Codex (or at least access to it).

| Approach | Check | Effect |
|----------|-------|--------|
| **Legal argument** | DC 14 Persuasion (Helena grants advantage with the charter log) | One success toward the three needed |
| **Scholarly testimony** | DC 12 History/Arcana (Arlen, Sella, or a PC) | One success; establishes public value |
| **Bribe** | 200 gp | Automatic success, but **+1 Censor's Shroud later** (note it, do not tick in Act I) |
| **Character witness** | DC 13 Persuasion/Insight (Helena, Tamsin, the captured Breaker) | One success; humanises the party |
| **Procedural delay** | DC 13 Investigation/Law | One success; buys time for Seraphel |

**Three successes before three failures.** On a failure, Renn adds a charge or raises the fine. On a total failure, Seraphel still arrives — but the party owes him more.

### 7.3 Seraphel's entrance

Before the verdict, **Lord Seraphel** arrives by carriage. He pays the clearance tax in full, cites the **Concord of Meridian** salvage precedent, and awards the party a **full fellowship to the Grand Lyceum, New Ilya** — letters, stipend, and a patron's protection.

He is warm, peer-like, and curious. He asks one piercing question about the mosaic lines and logs the answer privately. He never takes the Codex by force; he asks to *"sponsor authentication."*

**DM note:** this is staged benevolence. Seraphel is not saving them out of kindness. He is acquiring them.

### 7.4 The fellowship

- **100 gp stipend each.**
- **Letters of fellowship** to the Grand Lyceum.
- **Travel with Seraphel's retinue** to New Ilya (Act II hook).
- **A patron's attention** — flattering and dangerous.

## 13. Hand-Off to Act II

The chapter ends with the party on the road to **New Ilya**, carrying (or escorting) the **Catalogue Codex**, holding **Seraphel's fellowship letters**, and knowing three things:

1. The Ascensionists were **civic humanists**, not death-cultists.
2. Someone modern has been **looting and suppressing** their records.
3. The **transit map** shows connections across the continent — and someone wants it badly enough to kill for it.

**Act II opens with the Grand Lyceum audience** and the discovery that the archive's history has been *edited*. See `06_ADVENTURE/Act II/chapter_3_edited_past.md`.

## 15. Canon Guardrails

These are the lines Chapter 2 must not cross, checked against the bible, `01_CAMPAIGN/revelation_timeline.md`, `01_CAMPAIGN/milestone_progression.md`, and `08_MONSTERS_AND_ITEMS/`.

- **The Codex recontextualises, it does not re-confirm.** The Oath in Chapter 1 is the Act I confirmation of Ascensionist humanism. The Codex shows the *scale* of their ambition — continental, linked, systemic. It is the "answer generates two questions" beat, not a second proof.
- **The star-glass lens has no key function.** Canon is explicit (`relics_and_artifacts.md` §10). It can sharpen a view or focus a light, but it never opens a lock or solves a ring by itself. Vane buyers overpay for the story, not the function.
- **The Star-Glass Steward is not the Star-Glass Warden.** The CR 7 Warden is Nythara's stair guardian (`monsters_and_foes.md`). The CR 3 Steward is a lesser chamber-construct. Do not imply a shared origin yet.
- **The Returners are not named in Act I.** Seraphel's public front belongs to Act V. In Chapter 2 he offers access to "Ascendant scholars," not the Returners.
- **Seraphel is foreshadowed, not exposed.** The Lyceum letter is a procedural snub, not an accusation. The party should leave Act I trusting him, or at least owing him.
- **Level timing:** Session 4 ends at level 2; Session 5 ends at level 3; Session 6 ends at level 4. This matches the table's agreed three-night split and `milestone_progression.md`.
- **The Fourth Chair and the star-chart cylinder are optional hooks.** They are not required by the act plan. Use them only if the table enjoys the mystery; cut them if they compete with the Engine or the Tenth.
